## About this inscription

This small limestone fragment preserves seven incomplete lines. It was reported from Old Kandahar and published by André Dupont-Sommer in 1966. The text mixes Middle Indian phrases written in Aramaic letters with Aramaic explanations. Émile Benveniste identified the Indian phrases with a passage of Major Pillar Edict VII. Because every line has a broken beginning and some words remain obscure, there is no continuous translation of the complete original proclamation. The page gives **all surviving lines**, marking what can and cannot be interpreted; the full pillar text appears in the separate article on Edict VII.

## English translation

The following renders identifiable words and phrases rather than filling the lost portions. Some readings and meanings are disputed.

> 1. … “in accordance with” …
> 2. … “also, which” … [an Indian phrase corresponding to “whatever things indeed” in Pillar Edict VII] …
> 3. … “in accordance with” … “land” … “and now” … [an Indian phrase related to proper following or conduct] …
> 4. … “also, they have increased and will increase, in obedience” …
> 5. … “in accordance with … obedience” … [last word uncertain] …
> 6. … [an Indian phrase corresponding to *anuppatipatiyā*, “in accordance with” or “following”] …
> 7. … [uncertain word] … “slaves” [or another reading] …

Benveniste identified the Indian phrases in lines 2, 3, and 6 with wording that occurs in Major Pillar Edict VII, which has its own article in the guide. That identification gives context, but it does not restore the missing portions of this fragment.
