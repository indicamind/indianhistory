## About this inscription

This Greek inscription from Kandahar preserves the end of Major Rock Edict XII and the beginning of XIII. It is a separate Greek version of those messages, not a complete copy of either numbered edict. The first surviving lines ask people to control their speech when discussing other groups. The second part describes the conquest of Kalinga, the killing and deportation it caused, and the king’s remorse. The beginning and end of the stone are lost. The transcription below follows Francesco Maniscalco’s 2018 edition; square brackets mark editorial restorations, and the ellipsis marks the broken ending.

## English translation

This rendering covers the entire surviving Greek fragment. The text breaks off in mid-thought.

> [Reverence] and self-restraint are needed in every discussion. The strongest restraint is control of speech: one should neither praise one’s own group nor find fault with others without cause. Such speech is empty. One should instead try to praise others and avoid fault-finding in every way. In doing so, people improve themselves and win others over; those who disregard this become less honorable and alienate their neighbors. People who exalt themselves by blaming others, hoping to shine above them, harm themselves even more. They should respect one another and accept what others can teach. Sharing what each knows makes everyone wiser. Those who already follow these practices should keep hearing them so that their reverence may grow continually.
>
> In the eighth year of his reign, Piodasses conquered Kalinga. One hundred and fifty thousand people were captured and deported, another hundred thousand were killed, and nearly as many more died. From that time pity and compassion took hold of him, and he grieved deeply. He ordered restraint toward living beings and gave careful attention to reverence. The king found something still more distressing: among those who lived there were Brahmans, ascetics, and others devoted to reverence. They respected the king’s concerns, honored teachers, fathers, and mothers, loved friends and companions, spoke honestly to them, and treated slaves and hired workers gently.
