## About this inscription

The Karna Chaupar text dates a cave gift to the nineteenth year after King Priyadarśin’s consecration. Its damaged wording has led to different interpretations of the recipient and purpose. Hultzsch’s edition reads it as shelter for the rainy season; unlike the first two Barabar cave inscriptions, his surviving text does not name the Ājīvikas. It is the third text on the shared facsimile plate.

## Roman transliteration

The text below follows Hultzsch’s edition of the Karna Chaupar inscription. Square brackets mark restored or uncertain letters; dots mark a missing passage.

<p class="inscription-transliteration" lang="pra-Latn">1 lājā Piyadasī ekunavī2sati-vasā[bh]isi[t]e ja[lagh]o3[sagama]thāta [me] i[yaṃ kubhā] 4 su[p]i[y]e Kha .......... [di]5nā</p>

## English translation

This is the complete surviving text in E. Hultzsch’s 1925 English translation. The brackets reflect damaged passages in his edition.

> When king Priyadarśin had been anointed nineteen years, this cave in the very pleasant Kha[latika mountain] was given by me for (shelter during) the rainy season.
