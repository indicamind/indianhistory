## About this edict

The Rummindei pillar inscription identifies the place as the Buddha’s birthplace. Its damaged wording about taxation is interpreted in more than one way, so the exact fiscal measure remains debated.

## Roman transliteration

The text below follows the Lumbini version in Hultzsch’s edition. Square brackets show restored or uncertain letters; dots mark damaged or missing text. Other surviving copies can differ.

<p class="inscription-transliteration" lang="pra-Latn">(A) 1 Devāna[pi]yena Piyadasina lājina vīsati-vasābhisitena 2 atana āgāca mahīyite hida Budhe jāte Sakyamunī ti (B) 3 silā vigaḍabhī cā kālāpita silā-thabhe ca usapāpite 4 hida Bhagavaṃ jāte ti (C) Luṃmini-gāme ubalike kaṭe 5 aṭha-bhāgiye ca</p>

## English translation

This is E. Hultzsch’s 1925 translation of the same version. Its terminology and bracketed restorations reflect that historical edition.

> (A) When king Devānāṃpriya Priyadarśin had been anointed twenty years, he came himself and worshipped (this spot), because the Buddha Śākyamuni was born here.
> (B) (He) both caused to be made a stone bearing a horse(?) and caused a stone pillar to be set up, (in order to show) that the Blessed one was born here.
> (C) (He) made the village of Luṃmini free of taxes, and paying (only) an eighth share (of the produce).
