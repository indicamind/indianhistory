## About this edict

The third pillar edict turns attention from public gifts to personal self-scrutiny. The king warns that people tend to notice virtue in themselves and overlook wrongdoing.

## Roman transliteration

The text below follows the Delhi–Topra version in Hultzsch’s edition. Square brackets show restored or uncertain letters; dots mark damaged or missing text. Other surviving copies can differ.

<p class="inscription-transliteration" lang="pra-Latn">(A) 17 Devānaṃpiye Piyadasi lāja hevaṃ ahā (B) kayānaṃmeva dekhati iyaṃ me 18 kayāne kaṭe ti (C) no mina pāpaṃ d[e]khati iyaṃ me pāpe kaṭe ti iyaṃ vā āsinave 19 nāmā ti (D) dupaṭivekhe cu kho esā (E) hevaṃ cu kho esa dekhiye (F) imāni 20 āsinava-gāmīni nāma atha caṃḍiye niṭhūliye kodhe māne isyā 21 kālanena va hakaṃ mā palibhasayisaṃ (G) esa bāḍha dekhiye (H) iyaṃ me 22 hidatikāye iyaṃmana me pālatikāye</p>

## English translation

This is E. Hultzsch’s 1925 translation of the same version. Its terminology and bracketed restorations reflect that historical edition.

> (A) King Devānāṃpriya Priyadarśin speaks thus.
> (B) (Men) regard only (their) virtuous deeds, (thinking): ‘This virtuous deed has been performed by me.’
> (C) They do not at all regard (their) evil deeds, (thinking): ‘This evil deed has been performed by me; this very (act) is called a sin.’
> (D) Now this is indeed difficult to recognize.
> (E) But indeed this ought to be regarded thus:
> (F) ‘These (passions), viz. fierceness, cruelty, anger, pride, envy, are called sinful. Let me not ruin (myself) by (these) very (passions).’
> (G) The following ought to be specially-regarded:
> (H) ‘This (action conduces) to my (happiness) in this (world), that other (action) to my (happiness) in the other (world).’
