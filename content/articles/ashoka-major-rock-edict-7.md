## About this edict

This short edict values self-control and purity of mind across sects. It also warns that giving alone is insufficient without gratitude and disciplined conduct.

## Roman transliteration

The text below follows the Girnar version in Hultzsch’s edition. Square brackets show restored or uncertain letters; dots mark damaged or missing text. Other surviving copies can differ.

<p class="inscription-transliteration" lang="pra-Latn">(A) 1 Devānaṃpiyo Piyadasi rājā sarvata ichati save pāsaṃḍā vaseyu (B) save te sayamaṃ ca 2 bhava-sudhim ca ichati (C) jano tu ucāvaca-chamdo ucavaca-rago (D) te sarvaṃ va kāsaṃti eka-desaṃ vā kasa[ṃ]ti (E) 3 vip[ul]e tu pi dāne yasa nāsti sayame bhāva-sudhitā va kataṃñatā va daḍhabhati[t]ā ca nicā bāḍham</p>

## English translation

This is E. Hultzsch’s 1925 translation of the same version. Its terminology and bracketed restorations reflect that historical edition.

> (A) King Devānāṁpriya Priyadarśin desires (that) all sects may reside everywhere.
> (B) (For) all these desire both self-control and purity of mind.
> (C) But men possess various desires (and) various passions.
> (D) Either they will fulfil the whole, or they will fulfil (only) a portion (of their duties).
> (E) But even one who (practises) great liberality, (but) does not possess self-control, purity of mind, gratitude, and firm devotion, is very mean.
