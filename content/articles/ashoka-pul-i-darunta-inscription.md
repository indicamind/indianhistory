## About this inscription

The Pul-i Darunta, or Lampaka, stone was found near the entrance to Afghanistan’s Laghman valley. Its eight surviving lines combine Middle Indian phrases written in Aramaic letters with Aramaic explanations. W. B. Henning identified references to several Ashokan edicts, including passages associated with rock and pillar edicts, rather than one continuous proclamation. The stone is badly broken at the start of nearly every line, so a continuous translation is impossible. Its place in the Ashokan corpus rests on those textual correspondences; it is listed as an **associated inscription**.

## English translation

The breaks are too large to recover complete sentences. The following gives the sense of the surviving words and the source parallels Henning identified; suggested edict numbers are scholarly identifications, not numbers engraved on this stone.

> 1–2. … [damaged words concerning living beings and people] …
> 3. … “in accordance with” … [part of a cited Indian phrase] …
> 4. … “in accordance with” … [a phrase Henning compared with a rock edict] …
> 5. … “to see” … “in accordance with” … [another edict phrase] …
> 6. … “the Beloved of the Gods, consecrated” … “in accordance with” …
> 7. … “in accordance with” … “they ordered [it] to be written” …
> 8. … “others” … “made known” …

Henning connected the surviving phrases with several longer edicts, among them Major Rock Edict XIII. The fragment does not preserve those complete edicts; the pages for the numbered edicts give their separate surviving versions.
