## About this inscription

This Aramaic fragment was found at Taxila, reused in a later wall. Twelve short stretches of writing survive on a broken, once-polished stone. The phrase “our lord Priyadarśin” occurs twice, but the text does not say that the king ordered this particular inscription. Scholars have proposed different reconstructions, including parallels with Ashoka’s moral edicts. The fragments below are therefore presented as an **associated inscription** with a partial, cautious translation; the missing portions have not been silently supplied from other edicts.

## English translation

No uninterrupted translation is possible because the stone is broken. These are the discernible fragments, with disputed words marked as such:

> 1. … a record or memorial … [uncertain]
> 2. … for or concerning … [uncertain]
> 3–4. … harm or non-injury … [disputed]
> 5. … and to his father …
> 6. … good obedience; this …
> 7–8. … that … increase or good conduct … [disputed]
> 9. … our lord Priyadarśin …
> 10. … going or conduct … [disputed]
> 11. … and also his son or sons …
> 12. … of our lord Priyadarśin …

The language echoes themes found in Ashoka’s edicts, especially obedience and conduct. Those parallels do not establish that this broken stone was itself commissioned by him.
