Krishna describes renunciation and yoga, control of the self, preparation for meditation, posture, moderation, and repeated concentration on the self. Arjuna asks how the restless mind can be restrained and what happens to a practitioner whose yoga remains incomplete. Krishna answers through practice, dispassion and rebirth in favourable households, and ends by calling the faithful yogin devoted to him the most fully joined.

Each **Meaning** renders the Sanskrit in English. Each **Explanation** follows the verse’s statements, terms and argument. Named traditional interpretations are distinguished where they differ. [1–4]

## 6.1

### Meaning

The Lord said: One who performs the action that should be done without depending on its fruits is a renunciant and a yogin; merely giving up the sacred fire or ceasing activity does not make one so.

### Explanation

The sacred fire refers to the ritual responsibilities associated with a householder’s life. Giving it up can mark an outward change of religious condition, but Krishna makes freedom from dependence on fruits central to the present comparison. The verse carries Chapter 5’s account of inward renunciation into the discussion of meditation. The character of practice is established through what the person relinquishes in desire and identification.

## 6.2

### Meaning

What people call renunciation, know that to be yoga, Pandava; no one becomes a yogin without relinquishing desire-shaped intentions.

### Explanation

*Saṅkalpa* is intention or resolve, here associated with desired results. Krishna identifies renunciation with yoga and says that a person who has not renounced such intentions cannot become a yogin. Relinquishment is a necessary condition of yoga.

## 6.3

### Meaning

For a contemplative who wishes to ascend to yoga, action is said to be the means. For that same person established in yoga, stillness is said to be the means.

### Explanation

The verse distinguishes preparation from establishment. Action can purify and train the person who is beginning, while *śama*, quieting or stillness, has a central role once concentration has matured. The reference to “that same person” shows a development within a life of practice. It helps explain why Krishna can commend active discipline and then turn to a setting of withdrawal and sustained contemplation.

## 6.4

### Meaning

When one is attached neither to sense objects nor to actions, and has relinquished desire-shaped intentions, one is said to be established in yoga.

### Explanation

The person established in yoga is attached neither to sense objects nor to actions and has renounced all desire-shaped intentions. Krishna gives these forms of detachment as the marks of attainment. This defines the condition for which stillness is the means in 6.3.

## 6.5

### Meaning

One should raise the self through the self and not let the self sink down. The self alone is the self’s friend, and the self alone is the self’s enemy.

### Explanation

Krishna commands a person to raise the self through the self and not to let it fall. The repeated *ātman* can name the person and the faculties through which self-mastery is exercised. The self is called both friend and enemy according to whether it helps or obstructs this ascent.

## 6.6

### Meaning

For one who has mastered the self through the self, the self is a friend. But for one without self-mastery, the self behaves like an enemy.

### Explanation

The self is a friend to the person who has conquered it and an enemy to the person who has not. This explains the double description in 6.5. Mastery of the self determines whether it serves the person’s attainment or opposes it.

## 6.7

### Meaning

For one who has mastered the self and is peaceful, the supreme self is steadily present amid cold and heat, pleasure and pain, honour and dishonour.

### Explanation

The paired experiences include bodily conditions, felt enjoyment and suffering, and social judgment. Steadiness is considered across all three. The reference to *paramātman* gives the description its spiritual centre: peace is joined with establishment in the highest self. The yogin’s condition is therefore shown through what remains stable as circumstances and the opinions of others change.

## 6.8

### Meaning

A yogin satisfied by knowledge and realised understanding, steady and master of the senses, is called disciplined, seeing a lump of earth, a stone and gold with equal regard.

### Explanation

Knowledge and realised understanding satisfy this yogin. Steadiness and mastery of the senses are accompanied by equal regard for earth, stone and gold. Their different material values do not disturb the equanimity of the person established in yoga.

## 6.9

### Meaning

One whose understanding is even toward well-wishers, friends, enemies, the indifferent, mediators, those disliked, relatives, the good and wrongdoers is especially distinguished.

### Explanation

Krishna gives special distinction to the person whose understanding is equal toward every class named, including enemies, relatives, the virtuous and sinners. The list includes relationships that ordinarily produce attachment, hostility or partiality. The yogin’s understanding remains even across them.

## 6.10

### Meaning

A yogin should continually practise self-discipline in a secluded place, alone, with mind and self governed, free from expectation and possessive accumulation.

### Explanation

The yogin is to practise continually in seclusion and alone. Control of mind and self, absence of expectation and freedom from possessions are prescribed along with solitude. These are the conditions for the meditation detailed in the following verses.

## 6.11

### Meaning

In a clean place, one should establish a firm seat, neither too high nor too low, with cloth, a skin and kusha grass arranged for it.

### Explanation

The seat is to be firm, in a clean place and neither too high nor too low. Cloth, skin and kusha grass form its layers, with cloth above the skin and the grass beneath. Krishna specifies the physical preparation for the seated practice.

## 6.12

### Meaning

Seated there, making the mind one-pointed and governing the activities of mind and senses, one should practise yoga for inward purification.

### Explanation

On the prepared seat, the yogin makes the mind one-pointed and restrains the activities of mind and senses. The stated purpose is purification of the self. The instructions concern both the gathering of attention and control of the faculties during yoga.

## 6.13

### Meaning

Holding body, head and neck upright and steady, one should remain still, directing the gaze toward the tip of the nose and not looking around in different directions.

### Explanation

The body, head and neck are held upright, aligned and motionless. The yogin directs the gaze toward the tip of the nose and does not look about. These are the prescribed posture and direction of sight for the seated practice.

## 6.14

### Meaning

Peaceful, free from fear and firm in the vow of celibate discipline, governing the mind and directing thought toward me, the yogin should sit with me as the highest aim.

### Explanation

The vow of *brahmacarya* gives the passage an ascetic context in which conduct supports contemplation. Peacefulness and freedom from fear accompany the mind’s direction toward Krishna. The phrase *mat-para* makes that orientation explicit: the practice is centred on him as its highest aim. Posture and concentration are therefore joined with a way of living and a devotional commitment.

## 6.15

### Meaning

Continually practising in this way, with the mind governed, the yogin attains peace whose culmination is liberation and whose foundation is in me.

### Explanation

Continual practice with a controlled mind leads to peace culminating in liberation. Krishna identifies this peace as resting in him. The promised result belongs to the discipline of meditation, restraint and devotion described in 6.10–14.

## 6.16

### Meaning

Yoga is not for one who eats excessively, nor for one who does not eat at all, Arjuna; nor for one given to excessive sleep or continual wakefulness.

### Explanation

Krishna considers the rhythm of embodied life alongside formal meditation. Extremes of eating and sleeping can work against the steadiness the practice requires. The verse’s paired structure makes balance central to the discussion. This continues the chapter’s insistence that contemplation depends on a whole way of living, preparing the positive account of measured habits in the next verse.

## 6.17

### Meaning

For one whose food and recreation are measured, whose effort in actions is measured, and whose sleep and waking are measured, yoga becomes a destroyer of suffering.

### Explanation

*Yukta* means regulated or measured. Food, recreation, action, sleep and waking are all to be regulated. For the person who observes this measure, yoga destroys suffering. This supplies the positive rule corresponding to the rejection of excess and deprivation in 6.16.

## 6.18

### Meaning

When the thoroughly governed mind rests in the self alone, free from longing for all desired objects, a person is then said to be established in yoga.

### Explanation

The mind is completely restrained and established in the self alone. The person is free from longing for all objects of desire. Krishna identifies this as the condition of being joined in yoga, following the preparations and regulated conduct already prescribed.

## 6.19

### Meaning

A lamp standing in a windless place does not flicker: this is the comparison used for the yogin whose mind is governed while practising yoga of the self.

### Explanation

The flame remains luminous and steady when the currents that disturb it have quieted. The image describes the quality of attention associated with disciplined contemplation. Its force lies in stability rather than extinction: there is still light, but its motion no longer prevents steady illumination. The next verses elaborate what is disclosed when such gathered attention becomes established.

## 6.20

### Meaning

In that state the mind grows quiet, restrained through the practice of yoga; there, seeing the self through the self, one finds contentment in the self.

### Explanation

The mind becomes still through yogic restraint. The practitioner sees the self through the self and is satisfied in the self. These clauses describe the state of meditation continued through 6.23, where Krishna names its separation from suffering as yoga.

## 6.21

### Meaning

There one knows an ultimate happiness, grasped by understanding and beyond the senses; established in it, one does not depart from the truth.

### Explanation

Krishna distinguishes this happiness through the capacity by which it is apprehended. It is beyond sensory enjoyment and is reached through a clarified understanding. The second clause stresses stability in what has been recognised. This continues the sequence begun in 6.20, where quieting mind, seeing the self and inward contentment are connected as aspects of an established contemplative condition.

## 6.22

### Meaning

Having attained that, one considers no other gain greater. Established in it, one is not shaken even by great suffering.

### Explanation

After attaining this state, the yogin regards no other attainment as greater. Even severe suffering cannot shake the person established in it. Krishna states the completeness and firmness of the happiness described in 6.20–21.

## 6.23

### Meaning

Know that separation from association with suffering to be what is called yoga. This yoga should be practised with determination and a mind that does not lose heart.

### Explanation

Krishna defines yoga as separation, *viyoga*, from association with suffering. This completes the description of attainment in 6.20–22. The practice is to be undertaken with firm resolve and an undiscouraged mind.

## 6.24

### Meaning

Relinquishing completely all desires born from mental projections, one should govern the whole group of senses through the mind on every side.

### Explanation

*Saṅkalpa* names the mental intentions or conceptions from which desires arise. All desires born from them are to be abandoned without exception. The mind is then to restrain the entire group of senses on every side.

## 6.25

### Meaning

Little by little, one should become still through understanding held firm by resolve. Establishing the mind in the self, one should cease thinking of anything else.

### Explanation

The yogin is to become still gradually, guided by understanding held firm through resolve. Once the mind is established in the self, no other thought is to be entertained. The instruction prescribes the progressive cessation of outward-directed thought.

## 6.26

### Meaning

Wherever the restless and unsteady mind moves away, one should bring it back from there and direct it into the governance of the self alone.

### Explanation

Whenever the restless mind wanders, it is to be restrained and brought back under the self’s control. Krishna requires this return for every departure, whatever its object. The repeated restraint continues the gradual stilling described in 6.25.

## 6.27

### Meaning

The highest happiness comes to this yogin whose mind is peaceful, whose passion has quieted, who is free from stain and established in Brahman.

### Explanation

The highest happiness comes to the yogin whose mind is peaceful, whose *rajas* has subsided and who is free from sin. The person is described as having become Brahman. The verse joins these conditions with the attainment of supreme happiness.

## 6.28

### Meaning

Continually practising in this way, the yogin whose faults have fallen away readily experiences the limitless happiness of contact with Brahman.

### Explanation

The purified yogin who continues this practice easily experiences limitless happiness through contact with Brahman. *Brahma-saṃsparśa* names this contact. Its happiness is the result attributed to continuing yoga after the removal of sin or impurity.

## 6.29

### Meaning

The self joined to yoga, looking everywhere with equal vision, sees the self present in all beings and all beings present in the self.

### Explanation

The scope of contemplative vision now becomes universal. Traditional interpretations differ over its metaphysics: Shankara reads the passage through the identity of self and Brahman, while Ramanuja emphasises the essential likeness of selves apart from their embodied differences. The immediate movement is toward equal vision across beings, extending the equality described in Chapter 5. [3]

## 6.30

### Meaning

One who sees me everywhere and sees everything in me never loses sight of me, and I never lose sight of that person.

### Explanation

Krishna now speaks personally within the universal vision described in 6.29. Seeing him in all things and all things in him establishes a relationship that is no longer confined to one place or occasion. The paired assurance makes that relation reciprocal. The verse carries contemplation into a continuing recognition of divine presence within the world in which the yogin lives.

## 6.31

### Meaning

The yogin who worships me as present in all beings, established in unity, abides in me however that person may live and act.

### Explanation

The yogin worships Krishna as present in all beings and remains established in unity. Krishna says this person abides in him regardless of the manner of life or action. Worship and the vision of Krishna in every being define the person to whom the statement applies.

## 6.32

### Meaning

One who sees happiness and suffering everywhere with equal regard by comparison with the self is considered by me the highest yogin, Arjuna.

### Explanation

*Ātmaupamya* means comparison with oneself. The yogin sees the happiness and suffering of other beings as one’s own and regards them equally everywhere. Krishna ranks this person as the highest yogin.

## 6.33

### Meaning

Arjuna said: Madhusudana, I do not see how the yoga you have described through evenness can remain firmly established, because the mind is so restless.

### Explanation

Arjuna objects that the mind’s restlessness prevents the lasting establishment of the yoga of equality Krishna has described. He does not see how the proposed steadiness can be maintained. The following verse states the force of his objection through the comparison with wind.

## 6.34

### Meaning

For the mind is restless, Krishna, turbulent, powerful and obstinate. I consider governing it as difficult as governing the wind.

### Explanation

The accumulating descriptions give Arjuna’s difficulty weight. Mind changes direction, disrupts intentions, exerts force and persists in its habits. The wind image conveys something that seems to escape the person trying to contain it. This is a practical objection to the possibility of sustained contemplation, and Krishna’s response begins by accepting the reality of the difficulty.

## 6.35

### Meaning

The Lord said: Without doubt, mighty-armed one, the mind is restless and difficult to govern. But it is brought under discipline through repeated practice and dispassion, son of Kunti.

### Explanation

Krishna answers with agreement before identifying two complementary means. *Abhyāsa* is repeated practice, while *vairāgya* concerns loosening attachment to what pulls attention away. One develops the capacity to return; the other reduces the force of the competing demand. Together they make the account of discipline more precise, connecting the repeated return in 6.26 with the relinquishment described earlier in the chapter.

## 6.36

### Meaning

In my view, yoga is difficult to attain for one without self-restraint. But a self-governed person who strives can attain it through an appropriate means.

### Explanation

Krishna judges yoga difficult for the person without self-control. For the self-controlled person who strives, it can be attained through the proper means. The distinction makes restraint and effort conditions of attainment.

## 6.37

### Meaning

Arjuna said: What becomes of someone who has trust but insufficient perseverance, whose mind falls away from yoga before attaining its perfection, Krishna?

### Explanation

Arjuna asks about the fate of a person who has faith but fails to persevere. The mind turns away from yoga before perfection is reached. His question concerns what happens after this unfinished practice, which Krishna answers through an account of merit and rebirth.

## 6.38

### Meaning

Does such a person, fallen away from both ways, perish like a torn cloud, without a firm support and bewildered on the path to Brahman, mighty-armed one?

### Explanation

Arjuna fears the failed practitioner may lose both the fruits of worldly or Vedic action and the attainment sought through yoga. A torn cloud illustrates the destruction of one who has lost both supports. The person is described as bewildered on the path to Brahman.

## 6.39

### Meaning

Krishna, you should remove this doubt of mine completely, for no one other than you is able to resolve it.

### Explanation

Arjuna asks Krishna to remove the doubt completely and says no other person can do so. The doubt is the fate of the unsuccessful practitioner raised in 6.37–38. Krishna’s reply concerns that person’s destiny here, in other worlds and in future births.

## 6.40

### Meaning

The Lord said: Partha, there is no destruction for that person either here or hereafter. No one who does what is good goes toward an evil destiny, dear one.

### Explanation

Krishna denies that the unsuccessful practitioner is destroyed either in this world or hereafter. No one who does good reaches an evil destiny. This assurance answers Arjuna’s fear of losing both the fruits of action and the goal of yoga.

## 6.41

### Meaning

Having reached the worlds of those who act meritoriously and lived there for long ages, the one who fell short in yoga is born into a pure and prosperous household.

### Explanation

The unsuccessful yogin first reaches the worlds earned by meritorious action and remains there for a long time. After that residence, the person is reborn in a pure and wealthy household. Krishna describes heavenly reward followed by a favourable human birth.

## 6.42

### Meaning

Or such a person is born into a family of wise yogins. A birth of this kind is even more difficult to obtain in the world.

### Explanation

The other birth named is into a family of wise yogins. Krishna declares this birth rarer than the pure and prosperous household described in 6.41. It places the reborn practitioner among people already possessing yogic knowledge.

## 6.43

### Meaning

There the person regains the connection with understanding developed in the previous body and strives again toward perfection, descendant of Kuru.

### Explanation

In the new birth, the yogin regains the understanding acquired in the previous body and resumes striving for perfection. Krishna states that the earlier attainment carries across death and embodiment. The renewed effort builds on practice from the former life.

## 6.44

### Meaning

Through that earlier practice, the person is carried forward even without full control. Even one who seeks to know yoga goes beyond the domain of the sacred word.

### Explanation

Practice from the previous life draws the person onward, even involuntarily. Krishna further states that even the seeker who wishes to know yoga passes beyond *śabda-brahman*, the Vedic word and its prescribed ritual sphere. Earlier practice thus carries the seeker beyond ordinary ritual attainment.

## 6.45

### Meaning

But the yogin who strives earnestly, purified of faults and brought to maturity through many births, then reaches the highest destination.

### Explanation

The yogin who strives, is purified of sins and has attained maturity through many births reaches the highest destination. Krishna describes completion across repeated births together with the effort of the practitioner. This is the final outcome of the course resumed after an earlier failure.

## 6.46

### Meaning

The yogin is higher than practitioners of austerity, is considered higher than possessors of knowledge, and is higher than performers of ritual action. Therefore become a yogin, Arjuna.

### Explanation

Krishna praises yoga by comparison with several recognised disciplines. Commentators specify the knowledge being compared: Shankara identifies scriptural learning here, while Ramanuja describes knowledge other than knowledge of the self. The comparison therefore brings the stress onto the realised understanding developed in the chapter. Its purpose is to encourage Arjuna toward the practice whose preparation and fulfilment have just been explained. [4]

## 6.47

### Meaning

And among all yogins, the one who worships me with trust, with the inward self absorbed in me, is considered by me the most deeply joined in yoga.

### Explanation

The chapter closes by giving its highest description a devotional centre. The inward absorption developed through meditation is directed toward Krishna and expressed in trusting worship. This recalls the instruction to sit with him as the highest aim in 6.14 and the universal vision of 6.30–31. It also opens naturally into Chapter 7, where Krishna explains more fully how he is to be known.
