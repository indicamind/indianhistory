A bangle-covered arm, a rounded water pot, a pair of dice, a field crossed by furrows: some of the most vivid encounters with Harappan civilization begin with something that still looks familiar. This visual journey brings together 25 examples of lasting practices, recurring forms and cultural connections.

B. B. Lal’s 2010 address supplies the starting point. Museum photographs, excavation records and later scenes broaden the comparison across present-day India and Pakistan. Most ancient objects here belong to the third millennium BCE; each later image is labelled separately. Click a picture to enlarge it on this page. [1]

Look closely at what persists: a material, a tool, a bodily gesture or a way of working. The images bring ancient objects and later practices side by side, from ornaments and games to farming, craft and sacred imagery.

Harappan terracotta cart models preserve decisions about wheels, an axle, a load and animal traction. Kenoyer documents the long persistence of effective cart designs in India and Pakistan. The bronze shown here comes from Daimabad; its precise date and cultural affiliation are disputed. The later clay toy and the Madhya Pradesh cart extend the visual sequence through later settings. [5, 17]

## What these images carry forward

Continuity comes into view through particular details: a shell turned into a bracelet, a bead drilled through its centre, a cart designed for animal traction, clay raised on a wheel. Across these 25 comparisons, ancient objects sit beside later ornaments, tools, gestures and sacred images.

Together, they bring the everyday world of the past closer: things people wore, made, played with and passed on.
