Krishna describes the ancient transmission of yoga and his own repeated manifestations to protect the good, destroy wrongdoers and establish dharma. He explains action and non-action, several forms of sacrifice, and knowledge that burns away karma. Arjuna is instructed to obtain knowledge from those who have seen the truth and to cut through his doubt.

Each **Meaning** renders the Sanskrit in English. Each **Explanation** follows the verse’s statements, terms and argument. Named traditional interpretations are distinguished where they differ. [1–3]

## 4.1

### Meaning

The Lord said: I taught this enduring yoga to Vivasvan; Vivasvan taught it to Manu, and Manu spoke it to Ikshvaku.

### Explanation

Krishna says he taught this imperishable yoga to Vivasvan, the sun god. Vivasvan transmitted it to Manu, and Manu to Ikshvaku. The teaching is thus traced through divine and royal ancestors before being taught again to Arjuna.

## 4.2

### Meaning

Received through succession in this way, the royal sages knew this yoga. Over a long course of time, that yoga was lost here, scorcher of enemies.

### Explanation

The *rājarṣis*, rulers who are also sages, link understanding with responsibility for a community. Transmission depends on more than the continued existence of a set of words: the teaching must remain known and practised. Its loss explains why renewed instruction is necessary. Krishna’s account therefore makes continuity and interruption part of the history of spiritual understanding within the dialogue.

## 4.3

### Meaning

That same ancient yoga has been taught by me to you today, because you are my devotee and friend; this is a supreme secret.

### Explanation

Krishna teaches the same ancient yoga to Arjuna because Arjuna is both his devotee and his friend. He calls it the highest secret. The verse identifies their relationship as the reason Arjuna receives the teaching that had been lost from the line of transmission.

## 4.4

### Meaning

Arjuna said: Your birth is later, while Vivasvan’s birth was earlier. How am I to understand that you taught this in the beginning?

### Explanation

Arjuna asks a direct question about the chronology Krishna has just presented. His experience of Krishna as a contemporary does not readily fit a claim to have taught a primordial figure. The question gives the next passage its purpose: Krishna must explain the difference between his apparent human life and the divine identity he is about to disclose more fully.

## 4.5

### Meaning

The Lord said: Many births of mine and yours have passed, Arjuna. I know them all; you do not know them, scorcher of enemies.

### Explanation

Krishna answers within the chapter’s account of repeated birth, while identifying an asymmetry in knowledge. Both speaker and listener have a history extending beyond the present life, but Krishna remembers that history completely. The reply begins to distinguish divine manifestation from ordinary embodied existence. The next verse explains how Krishna can speak of birth while also claiming to be unborn and imperishable.

## 4.6

### Meaning

Although unborn, of imperishable being and Lord of creatures, I manifest through my own power, presiding over my own nature.

### Explanation

The verse places divine freedom alongside manifestation in the world. *Ātma-māyā* names Krishna’s own power in this act. Traditional readings describe its nature differently: Shankara stresses manifestation as appearing embodied through divine māyā, while Ramanuja emphasises the Lord’s freely chosen manifestation without loss of divine qualities. Both distinguish this appearance from ordinary birth under the pressure of karma. [3]

## 4.7

### Meaning

Whenever dharma declines and adharma rises, descendant of Bharata, then I bring myself forth.

### Explanation

Krishna declares that he brings himself forth whenever dharma declines and adharma rises. His manifestation responds to the deterioration of righteousness and prescribed order. The statement explains when the divine manifestation described in 4.6 occurs.

## 4.8

### Meaning

For the protection of the good, the destruction of wrongdoers and the re-establishment of dharma, I manifest age after age.

### Explanation

Krishna names three purposes of his recurring manifestation: protection of the good, destruction of wrongdoers and restoration of dharma. “Age after age” states that these divine manifestations recur.

## 4.9

### Meaning

One who truly knows my divine birth and action does not undergo another birth after leaving the body, but comes to me, Arjuna.

### Explanation

Understanding Krishna’s birth requires grasping the freedom and purpose described in the preceding verses. The knowledge concerns the character of his action as well as the fact of his manifestation. Its promised result is release from renewed birth and arrival at Krishna. The verse thus connects theological understanding with the chapter’s continuing question of how action can be free from binding consequences.

## 4.10

### Meaning

Freed from attachment, fear and anger, absorbed in me and taking refuge in me, many have been purified by the discipline of knowledge and have attained my state.

### Explanation

The teaching joins understanding, purification and devotion. Attachment, fear and anger are the disturbances already examined in earlier chapters; here they are overcome in a life oriented toward Krishna. *Jñāna-tapas* gives knowledge the character of a purifying discipline. “Many” presents the possibility as one realised by earlier seekers, linking Arjuna’s present instruction with an established path of transformation.

## 4.11

### Meaning

In whatever way people approach me, I respond to them accordingly. In every way, Partha, people follow my path.

### Explanation

Krishna responds to people according to the manner in which they approach him. He also declares that people follow his path in every way. Different approaches receive corresponding results; the next verse identifies quick success in action as one such aim.

## 4.12

### Meaning

Those who desire success in their actions worship the gods here, for success born of action comes quickly in the human world.

### Explanation

People who want success in action worship the gods because action produces success quickly in the human world. Krishna states the efficacy of this worship and identifies its aim as success, in contrast with the liberation discussed in the surrounding verses.

## 4.13

### Meaning

The fourfold varna order was created by me according to the divisions of qualities and actions. Although its creator, know me as the unchanging non-doer.

### Explanation

The verse attributes the fourfold social order to Krishna and names *guṇa* and *karma*, qualities and actions, as its differentiating principles. Chapter 18 later sets out the four groups and their duties. Here the statement also serves the argument about agency: Krishna claims a creative role while remaining unchanged and unbound by action. Social ordering and the question of divine non-doership meet in a single sentence.

## 4.14

### Meaning

Actions do not stain me, and I have no longing for their fruits. One who understands me in this way is also not bound by actions.

### Explanation

Krishna says actions do not stain him because he has no desire for their fruits. The person who knows him in this way is likewise not bound by action. Knowledge of Krishna’s freedom from action’s effects is therefore said to free the knower from those bonds.

## 4.15

### Meaning

Knowing this, earlier seekers of liberation also performed action. Therefore you too should act, as those in earlier times acted.

### Explanation

Earlier seekers of liberation knew this freedom from bondage and nevertheless performed action. Krishna commands Arjuna to act as they did. Their example supports the continued performance of action by one who seeks liberation.

## 4.16

### Meaning

Even the discerning are confused about what action is and what inaction is. I will explain action to you; knowing it, you will be freed from what is harmful.

### Explanation

Krishna acknowledges that outward observation alone does not settle the issue. Bodily movement, abstention and inward attachment can be combined in different ways. This makes the ethical and spiritual character of an act harder to judge than its appearance suggests. The promised explanation concerns the understanding through which action ceases to bind, rather than merely a list of activities to perform or avoid.

## 4.17

### Meaning

Action must be understood, and so must wrong action and inaction. The course of action is difficult to penetrate.

### Explanation

The three terms broaden the inquiry. Prescribed or appropriate action, *vikarma* or action contrary to what should be done, and *akarma* or inaction cannot be sorted solely by visible effort. Their course includes purpose, understanding and consequences. Krishna prepares Arjuna for the paradox in the next verse, where the same outward behaviour can be seen differently from the standpoint of bondage and freedom.

## 4.18

### Meaning

One who sees inaction in action and action in inaction is wise among people; that person is disciplined and has accomplished all action.

### Explanation

The wise person sees inaction within action and action within inaction. The distinction concerns the true character of agency rather than the mere presence or absence of bodily movement. The following verses describe the knower who continues to act while free from desire, attachment and the binding effects of action.

## 4.19

### Meaning

Those whose undertakings are all free from desire-driven intentions, and whose actions have been burned by the fire of knowledge, are called wise by the discerning.

### Explanation

The undertakings of the wise are free from desires and the intentions formed around them. Knowledge burns their actions as fire burns fuel, destroying their capacity to bind. These are the conditions by which Krishna identifies the person whom the discerning call wise.

## 4.20

### Meaning

Having abandoned attachment to the fruits of action, always content and depending on no external support for fulfilment, one does nothing at all even while fully engaged in action.

### Explanation

The person has relinquished attachment to action’s fruits, is always content and depends on nothing. Krishna says that even while fully engaged in action, this person does nothing at all. The verse states non-doership alongside continuing outward activity, extending the distinction between action and inaction introduced in 4.18.

## 4.21

### Meaning

Free from expectation, with mind and self governed and all possessiveness relinquished, one incurs no fault while doing only what sustains bodily life.

### Explanation

The person has relinquished expectation and possessions and brought mind and self under control. Activity continues only for the maintenance of the body. Krishna declares that this person incurs no sin through such action, continuing the account of action that does not bind.

## 4.22

### Meaning

Content with what comes of itself, beyond opposing experiences and free from envy, even-minded in success and failure, one is not bound although acting.

### Explanation

Several familiar sources of agitation are brought together: acquisition, painful and pleasant contrasts, comparison with others, and the demand for success. Contentment changes the person’s relation to each. The sentence again retains action while describing freedom from its bonds. This repeated combination gives substance to Krishna’s teaching: liberation concerns the way action is understood and undertaken within changing circumstances.

## 4.23

### Meaning

For one whose attachment has gone, who is free, whose mind is established in knowledge and who acts as an offering, action dissolves completely.

### Explanation

The language of *yajña*, introduced in Chapter 3, now meets the language of knowledge. The action is given over as an offering rather than owned as a private achievement. Its dissolution concerns the binding accumulation described throughout the passage. This verse also introduces the chapter’s next movement, in which the structure of sacrifice becomes a way to understand many kinds of spiritual discipline.

## 4.24

### Meaning

The offering instrument is Brahman; the offering is Brahman; it is offered by Brahman into the fire of Brahman. Brahman is the destination of one whose attention rests on action as Brahman.

### Explanation

Every element of the sacrifice is identified with Brahman: the instrument of offering, the substance offered, the agent, the fire and the act. The person absorbed in action as Brahman reaches Brahman. This is the vision of sacrifice expressed through knowledge.

## 4.25

### Meaning

Some yogins attend to sacrifice offered to the gods; others offer sacrifice itself through sacrifice into the fire of Brahman.

### Explanation

Some yogins offer sacrifice to the gods. Others offer sacrifice into the fire of Brahman, continuing the identification of sacrificial elements with Brahman in 4.24. Krishna begins a list of forms of sacrifice that includes ritual offerings, restraint, study and control of the vital breaths.

## 4.26

### Meaning

Some offer the senses, beginning with hearing, into the fires of restraint; others offer objects, beginning with sound, into the fires of the senses.

### Explanation

The senses themselves are offered into the fires of restraint in one sacrifice. In another, sound and the other sense objects are offered into the fires of the senses. The verse uses the structure of sacrifice to describe restraint of sensory activity and the reception of objects by the senses.

## 4.27

### Meaning

Others offer all the activities of the senses and the life-breaths into the fire of self-restraint through yoga, kindled by knowledge.

### Explanation

All the activities of the senses and the vital breaths are offered into the fire of yogic self-restraint. Knowledge kindles this fire. The sacrifice encompasses the sensory and vital operations together, rather than one sense or one object alone.

## 4.28

### Meaning

Others offer material goods, austerity or yoga; still others, striving with firm vows, offer study and knowledge.

### Explanation

The sacrifices named here consist of material goods, austerity, yoga, sacred study and knowledge. The practitioners are ascetics who strive and observe firm vows. These forms belong to the catalogue of sacrifices whose purifying effect Krishna states in 4.30.

## 4.29

### Meaning

Some devoted to regulation of breath offer prana into apana, and others apana into prana, holding the movements of prana and apana in restraint.

### Explanation

The traditional names *prāṇa* and *apāna* identify vital movements used in the account of breath regulation. The verse presents their relation as an offering, bringing bodily discipline within the chapter’s wider language of sacrifice. Breath becomes part of what is consciously governed and given over. This continues the catalogue of practices whose significance will be gathered into the discussion of knowledge.

## 4.30

### Meaning

Others, disciplined in food, offer the life-breaths into the life-breaths. All these understand sacrifice and have their impurities worn away through sacrifice.

### Explanation

Some regulate their food and offer the vital breaths into the vital breaths. Krishna then describes all the practitioners in the catalogue as knowers of sacrifice whose sins or impurities are destroyed through sacrifice. Purification is attributed to the sacrifices they perform.

## 4.31

### Meaning

Those who partake of the nectar remaining from sacrifice reach eternal Brahman. For one who offers no sacrifice, even this world is not fulfilled; how then another, best of the Kurus?

### Explanation

Those who eat the nectar left after sacrifice attain eternal Brahman. Krishna denies even this world to the person who performs no sacrifice and asks how such a person could attain another world. Sacrifice is presented as necessary for worldly and otherworldly attainment.

## 4.32

### Meaning

Thus many forms of sacrifice are spread out in sacred revelation. Know them all to arise through action; understanding them in this way, you will be released.

### Explanation

The many sacrifices are set forth in the Veda and all arise from action. Krishna tells Arjuna to know their origin in action and promises release through this knowledge. The catalogue establishes the range of sacrificial activity before knowledge is ranked above material sacrifice.

## 4.33

### Meaning

The sacrifice of knowledge is higher than sacrifice consisting of material goods, scorcher of enemies. Every action, without exception, finds its fulfilment in knowledge, Partha.

### Explanation

Krishna explicitly ranks the sacrifice of knowledge above sacrifice made with material goods. All action reaches completion in knowledge. The verse establishes a hierarchy among the sacrifices just listed and gives knowledge the highest place.

## 4.34

### Meaning

Learn this through prostration, searching questions and service. Those who know and have seen the truth will teach you that knowledge.

### Explanation

Arjuna is to approach those who know the truth with prostration, questions and service. These teachers will impart knowledge because they have seen the truth. Submission to the teacher and inquiry are both included among the required means of learning.

## 4.35

### Meaning

Knowing this, you will not again fall into such confusion, Pandava. Through it you will see all beings without exception in the self, and then in me.

### Explanation

The knowledge received from the teachers removes the delusion afflicting Arjuna. Through it he will see all beings in the self and then in Krishna. Krishna promises a vision of the relation among beings, self and himself, together with freedom from renewed confusion.

## 4.36

### Meaning

Even if you were the greatest wrongdoer among all wrongdoers, you would cross over all wrongdoing by the raft of knowledge.

### Explanation

Knowledge is compared to a raft that carries a person across all sin. Krishna states that this applies even to the greatest sinner among sinners. The verse attributes this power to knowledge without placing the person’s accumulated wrongdoing beyond its reach.

## 4.37

### Meaning

As a blazing fire turns fuel into ashes, Arjuna, so the fire of knowledge turns all actions into ashes.

### Explanation

The fire image returns from 4.19 with its transformative force made explicit. Fuel ceases to function as fuel once consumed; similarly, knowledge destroys the binding efficacy attributed to action in the argument. The verse concerns the realisation developed through the chapter, including the relinquishment of egoic ownership. Its language explains why Krishna gives knowledge such a central place among the many forms of sacrifice.

## 4.38

### Meaning

There is no purifier in this world comparable to knowledge. One perfected through yoga comes to find it within the self in time.

### Explanation

Krishna declares knowledge the greatest purifier in the world. A person perfected through yoga finds this knowledge in the self in due course. Yoga prepares the practitioner, and the knowledge attained within the self purifies.

## 4.39

### Meaning

A person with trust, devoted to the aim and governing the senses, attains knowledge. Having attained it, that person soon reaches the highest peace.

### Explanation

Faith, dedication and control of the senses are the conditions for obtaining knowledge. Once knowledge is attained, supreme peace follows quickly. Krishna links the promised peace to these disciplines and to the knowledge they make possible.

## 4.40

### Meaning

The ignorant person who lacks faith and is full of doubt perishes. For such a doubting person there is neither this world nor the next, nor happiness.

### Explanation

The ignorant, faithless and doubting person perishes. Krishna denies this person happiness, this world and the next. The warning contrasts with the promise of knowledge and peace made to the faithful person in 4.39.

## 4.41

### Meaning

Actions do not bind the self-governed person who has relinquished actions through yoga and whose doubts have been cut apart by knowledge, Dhananjaya.

### Explanation

Actions do not bind the person who has renounced them through yoga, cut through doubt with knowledge and mastered the self. Yoga and knowledge together remove the conditions of bondage. *Ātmavant* describes the person who possesses self-mastery.

## 4.42

### Meaning

Therefore cut down, with the sword of knowledge, this doubt in your heart that has arisen from ignorance. Stand in yoga and rise, descendant of Bharata.

### Explanation

Krishna commands Arjuna to cut apart the doubt born of ignorance with the sword of knowledge. He is then to take his stand in yoga and rise. The command returns the teaching on knowledge and unbound action to Arjuna’s situation on the battlefield.
