Krishna describes his greatness and the understanding he gives his devotees. Arjuna asks which forms and manifestations he should contemplate. Krishna names examples among gods, sages, animals, rulers, natural phenomena, words and human qualities, including kingship, punishment and gambling. He ends by saying that he sustains the whole world with a single portion of himself.

Each **Meaning** renders the Sanskrit in English. Each **Explanation** follows the verse’s statements, terms and argument. Named traditional interpretations are distinguished where they differ. [1–3]

## 10.1

### Meaning

Krishna said: Hear once more my highest word, mighty-armed one. Because you listen with delight, I will speak to you with a desire for your welfare.

### Explanation

The renewed invitation treats Arjuna's listening as part of the relationship through which instruction continues. Krishna names concern for the listener's good as his motive, while acknowledging the delight the teaching has awakened. This opening prepares a chapter in which knowledge and devotion reinforce one another. The examples to come are intended to shape how Arjuna recognises the world and its source in continuing experience.

## 10.2

### Meaning

The hosts of gods and the great sages do not know my origin, for I am in every way the origin of the gods and the great sages.

### Explanation

Even beings associated with exceptional knowledge are situated within the dependence Krishna describes. Their inability to know his origin follows from his priority as their source. The claim extends beyond human limitations, giving theological force to the coming catalogue of divine and sage figures. Krishna's manifestations can be recognised among them while his reality remains prior to the existence and capacities of any particular member.

## 10.3

### Meaning

Whoever among mortals knows me as unborn, without beginning and the great Lord of the worlds is free from delusion and released from all wrongdoing.

### Explanation

The person who knows Krishna as unborn, beginningless and the great Lord of the worlds is freed from delusion and all wrongdoing. The three descriptions distinguish him from the gods and sages whose origin he claims to be. Knowing him in truth therefore has a stated result for mortals: release from the sins that bind them.

## 10.4

### Meaning

Understanding, knowledge, freedom from delusion, patience, truthfulness, control of the senses, calm of mind, pleasure, pain, becoming, passing away, fear and fearlessness—

### Explanation

This list continues into the next verse. Understanding, knowledge and freedom from delusion appear alongside restraint, patience and truthfulness; pleasure, pain, birth, death, fear and fearlessness are included as well. All receive the same predicate in 10.5: these varied conditions of beings arise from Krishna alone. He claims to be their source across their opposing forms.

## 10.5

### Meaning

Non-harming, equality, contentment, austerity, giving, honour and dishonour: these varied conditions of beings arise from me alone.

### Explanation

Non-harming, equality, contentment, austerity, giving, honour and dishonour complete the list. Krishna says that all these varied conditions of beings arise from him alone. The declaration encompasses both virtues and opposed experiences such as honour and dishonour, continuing the pairs of pleasure and pain, fear and fearlessness in the preceding verse.

## 10.6

### Meaning

The seven great sages, the four ancient ones, and the Manus, whose descendants are these people of the world, were born from my mind, endowed with my being.

### Explanation

The world's origin is described through its ancestral and sage lineages. The Sanskrit grouping has traditional interpretive differences: “the four ancient ones” can be connected with the Manus or understood as an additional group of primordial sages. The shared point is the dependence of these progenitors on Krishna's mind and power. The verse presents sacred genealogy as another way to contemplate the divine source of the living world. [3]

## 10.7

### Meaning

Whoever truly knows this manifestation and yoga of mine becomes joined through unwavering yoga. Of this there is no doubt.

### Explanation

True knowledge of Krishna's vibhūti and yoga brings unwavering yoga to the knower. Vibhūti names his divine manifestations, and yoga his power or sovereign activity. Knowledge of these has the stated effect of firmly joining the practitioner to him. Krishna closes the declaration by excluding doubt about this result.

## 10.8

### Meaning

I am the origin of everything; everything proceeds from me. Understanding this, the wise worship me with a devoted disposition.

### Explanation

The relation between knowledge and worship is stated directly. The wise recognise Krishna as the source and continuing ground of all that proceeds, and that understanding takes the form of devotion. Bhāva gives the statement an affective depth: their recognition shapes the disposition with which they live. The verse thus explains why the chapter can move readily from claims about the world's origin to descriptions of loving religious practice.

## 10.9

### Meaning

With minds directed to me and lives devoted to me, enlightening one another and speaking continually of me, they find contentment and delight.

### Explanation

The devotees direct their minds and their lives toward Krishna. They teach one another about him and speak of him continually, finding satisfaction and delight in doing so. The verse specifies the activities of the wise worshippers named in 10.8. Their knowledge that everything proceeds from Krishna is accompanied by continuing conversation, instruction and joy in him.

## 10.10

### Meaning

To those who are continually dedicated and worship me with love, I give the yoga of understanding through which they come to me.

### Explanation

Buddhi-yoga, introduced earlier as a discipline of understanding, is now described as a gift from Krishna to loving devotees. Their continued dedication and the divine response are presented together. The verse gives a personal account of how the understanding needed for attainment becomes available. It connects the seeker's practice with divine initiative, rather than treating insight as an achievement wholly detached from devotion.

## 10.11

### Meaning

Out of compassion for them, dwelling within their own being, I destroy the darkness born of ignorance with the shining lamp of knowledge.

### Explanation

The lamp image makes knowledge an illuminating presence within the devotee's life. Krishna speaks of destroying ignorance from within, giving the promised buddhi-yoga an intimate setting. Compassion supplies the motive for the action. The contrast of light and darkness is familiar to the chapter's coming examples, but here the concern is the person's understanding: the source of the world is also presented as the one who makes recognition possible.

## 10.12

### Meaning

Arjuna said: You are the supreme Brahman, the supreme abode, the highest purifier, the eternal divine Person, the primordial deity, unborn and all-pervading—

### Explanation

Arjuna begins an affirmation continued in the next verse. The titles gather up much of what Krishna has taught about imperishability, sourcehood and the highest goal. They also reveal how far the conversation has moved from the initial picture of a warrior addressing his charioteer. Arjuna is identifying the person before him with the comprehensive divine reality described in the teaching.

## 10.13

### Meaning

So all the sages proclaim you: the divine sage Narada, Asita, Devala and Vyasa. You yourself also tell me this.

### Explanation

Arjuna places Krishna's self-description beside the testimony of recognised sages. The named figures establish a setting of inherited religious authority, while the ongoing conversation supplies direct instruction. His affirmation does not end his questioning; the next verses turn it into a request for ways of contemplating what he has accepted. Trust in testimony and the wish for fuller understanding therefore remain connected within the dialogue.

## 10.14

### Meaning

I hold everything you tell me to be true, Keshava. Neither the gods nor the danavas know your manifestation, Lord.

### Explanation

The acknowledgement echoes Krishna's earlier statement about the limits of divine and sage knowledge. Arjuna accepts the instruction while recognising that the teacher's manifestation exceeds the grasp of powerful cosmic beings. This creates the basis for the request that follows: the one whose nature cannot be fully inferred from outside must explain how he can be recognised. The verse expresses trust together with an awareness of the scope of the inquiry.

## 10.15

### Meaning

You alone know yourself through yourself, highest Person, source of beings, Lord of beings, God of gods and Lord of the world.

### Explanation

The repeated addresses describe Krishna through the relationships already developed in the chapter. Source, lordship and supremacy over divine beings combine with the claim of self-knowledge. Arjuna's words acknowledge a limit to ordinary observation while asking the speaker to disclose himself. The point is significant to the narrative: the further teaching and the vision of Chapter 11 will arise through an act of divine self-revelation.

## 10.16

### Meaning

You should fully describe your divine manifestations, by which you pervade and remain throughout these worlds.

### Explanation

Arjuna asks for the many forms through which the comprehensive presence can be contemplated. The request concerns how Krishna pervades the worlds, giving concrete direction to the earlier abstract claims about sourcehood. “Fully” expresses the extent of Arjuna's desire to hear, while Krishna will soon clarify that any spoken list must be selective. The tension between inexhaustible reality and finite description shapes the chapter's catalogue.

## 10.17

### Meaning

How may I know you, yogin, by continually contemplating you? In which particular conditions or forms should I think of you, Lord?

### Explanation

Arjuna asks how to know Krishna through continual contemplation and which particular manifestations should be its objects. He wants Krishna to name the forms in which he should be remembered. The question leads to the catalogue of gods, sages, creatures, powers and natural phenomena, each identified as a divine manifestation within its class.

## 10.18

### Meaning

Tell me again in detail of your yoga and manifestation, Janardana, for I find no satiation in hearing your nectar-like words.

### Explanation

Arjuna's eagerness gives an affective setting to his request for knowledge. The imagery of nectar describes the sustaining delight of listening, while the desire for detail keeps inquiry active. The chapter began with Krishna recognising this delight, and Arjuna now confirms it in his own speech. The exchange makes understanding, trust and enjoyment of the teaching mutually reinforcing parts of the developing devotional relationship.

## 10.19

### Meaning

Krishna said: Yes, I shall describe to you my divine manifestations in their chief examples, best of the Kurus, for there is no end to my extent.

### Explanation

Krishna agrees to describe his chief divine manifestations. He states that his extent has no end, so the account will name principal examples. This answers Arjuna's request for the particular forms through which to contemplate him. The same inexhaustibility is repeated at the catalogue's close, before Krishna states the general relation between every distinguished manifestation and his radiance.

## 10.20

### Meaning

I am the self dwelling in the hearts of all beings, Gudakesha. I am their beginning, their middle and also their end.

### Explanation

The first manifestation is inward and universal. Krishna is identified with the self present in all beings and with the entire course of their existence. This opening gives a foundation for the more differentiated examples: they are considered within a reality that already concerns every being. The language of heart or inner dwelling also connects the cosmic catalogue with the personal illumination described in verse 10.11.

## 10.21

### Meaning

Among the Adityas I am Vishnu; among lights, the radiant sun. Among the Maruts I am Marichi, and among the lunar mansions or stars, the moon.

### Explanation

Among the Adityas Krishna names Vishnu, among lights the radiant sun, among the Maruts Marichi, and among nakṣatras the moon. Nakṣatra refers to the stars or lunar mansions. Each group receives one named identification with Krishna. The verse begins the detailed catalogue after his universal declaration that he is the self in every being and their beginning, middle and end.

## 10.22

### Meaning

Among the Vedas I am the Sama Veda; among the gods, Vasava, Indra. Among the senses I am mind, and among living beings I am consciousness.

### Explanation

Sacred song, divine sovereignty, coordination of the senses and living awareness are placed beside one another. The Sama Veda gives a ritual and musical form to sacred utterance, while mind gathers the senses' activity into experience. Consciousness then names a pervasive feature of living beings. The sequence broadens the field of recognition, showing that the teaching moves through both highly distinguished figures and functions present within ordinary embodied life.

## 10.23

### Meaning

Among the Rudras I am Shankara; among yakshas and rakshasas, the lord of wealth. Among the Vasus I am fire, and among mountains with peaks, Meru.

### Explanation

Shankara is Shiva, named here among the Rudras. The lord of wealth among yakshas and rakshasas is Kubera. Fire is selected among the Vasus, and Meru among peaked mountains. The verse identifies Krishna with these particular gods and with the cosmic mountain, continuing the account of his chief manifestations within the divine and natural worlds.

## 10.24

### Meaning

Among household priests, know me as the chief, Brihaspati, Partha. Among army commanders I am Skanda; among bodies of water I am the ocean.

### Explanation

Brihaspati is named as the foremost household priest, Skanda among commanders and the ocean among bodies of water. Priesthood, command in war and the vastness of the sea supply three distinct classes of manifestation. Krishna tells Arjuna to know him through the principal figure in each class, as he has agreed to describe at the catalogue's opening.

## 10.25

### Meaning

Among the great sages I am Bhrigu; among utterances, the single syllable Om. Among sacrifices I am the sacrifice of recitation; among immovable things, the Himalaya.

### Explanation

The sequence links a sage, a concentrated sacred sound, a repeated devotional act and a mountain range. Japa-yajña places recitation within the language of sacrifice, connecting an accessible practice with the larger ritual framework. Om condenses sacred utterance into one syllable, while the Himalaya expresses enduring grandeur through place. The examples give contemplation both inwardly repeatable and outwardly recognisable supports.

## 10.26

### Meaning

Among all trees I am the ashvattha; among divine sages, Narada. Among gandharvas I am Chitraratha; among perfected beings, the sage Kapila.

### Explanation

A tree and three distinguished figures bring together sacred landscape, seership, celestial artistry and perfected knowledge. The ashvattha, familiar as the pipal tree, will become the central image of Chapter 15 in a different context. Here it is one selected manifestation among trees. The verse's named examples retain their particular identities while serving the shared purpose of directing attention toward Krishna through what is especially honoured or accomplished.

## 10.27

### Meaning

Among horses, know me as Ucchaihshravas, born with the nectar of immortality; among lordly elephants, Airavata; and among people, the king.

### Explanation

Ucchaihshravas is the horse born when the gods and asuras churned the ocean for the nectar of immortality. Airavata, named among lordly elephants, belongs to the same account. Among human beings Krishna identifies himself with the king. The verse joins these exceptional animals with the ruler who holds the highest place in the human political order.

## 10.28

### Meaning

Among weapons I am the thunderbolt; among cows, the wish-fulfilling cow. I am Kandarpa, the power of generation, and among serpents I am Vasuki.

### Explanation

Force, abundance, generation and serpent sovereignty provide distinct forms of power. The thunderbolt carries martial force, the wish-fulfilling cow expresses sustaining plenty, and Kandarpa names desire in its generative aspect. This last example recalls the qualified recognition of desire in verse 7.11. The verse works through the religious and mythic associations of its examples, placing their characteristic capacities within Krishna's account of divine manifestation.

## 10.29

### Meaning

Among nagas I am Ananta; among beings of the waters, Varuna. Among ancestors I am Aryaman; among those who restrain, I am Yama.

### Explanation

Krishna names Ananta among nagas and Varuna among beings of the waters. Aryaman is his identification among the ancestors, while Yama is named among those who restrain. The verse continues the serpent imagery of Vasuki in 10.28 and extends the catalogue through water, ancestral beings and the power of restraint. These named rulers and powers are presented as his manifestations.

## 10.30

### Meaning

Among the daityas I am Prahlada; among those who reckon, I am time. Among animals I am the lion, and among birds, the son of Vinata.

### Explanation

Prahlada is named among daityas, time among those who reckon, the lion among animals and Garuda, the son of Vinata, among birds. Prahlada is a devotee born within the daitya lineage. The four identifications include a revered person, the measure governing reckoning and the foremost animal and bird named in their classes. Krishna claims each as his manifestation.

## 10.31

### Meaning

Among purifiers I am the wind; among bearers of weapons, Rama. Among creatures of the waters I am the makara, and among streams I am Jahnavi, the Ganga.

### Explanation

Krishna identifies himself with wind among purifiers, Rama among bearers of weapons, the makara among aquatic creatures and Jahnavi, the Ganga, among rivers. Makara names a great water creature whose identification varies in traditional accounts. Jahnavi is the Ganga's name associated with the sage Jahnu. The verse gives each its place in the catalogue of principal manifestations.

## 10.32

### Meaning

Of creations I am the beginning, the end and the middle, Arjuna. Among fields of knowledge I am knowledge of the self; among those who debate, I am reasoned discussion.

### Explanation

The verse returns to the entire course of existence, then identifies two forms of understanding. Adhyātma-vidyā gives knowledge of the self a central place, consistent with the Gita's inquiry into action and liberation. Vāda points toward reasoned discussion aimed at establishing what is true. Its presence in the catalogue gives reflective exchange, including the dialogue itself, a place among the manifestations of divine excellence.

## 10.33

### Meaning

Among letters I am the sound A; among compounds, the coordinate compound. I am inexhaustible time, and the sustainer whose faces are everywhere.

### Explanation

Language becomes a field of contemplation through its elementary sound and its forms of combination. The coordinate compound, dvandva, joins terms while preserving their paired or grouped standing. The verse then moves from linguistic structure to inexhaustible time and all-facing support. The range is deliberate: both the articulation of speech and the encompassing conditions of existence can direct thought toward the sustaining presence Krishna describes.

## 10.34

### Meaning

I am death, which takes everything away, and the arising of those yet to be. Among feminine powers I am fame, prosperity, speech, memory, intelligence, steadfastness and forgiveness.

### Explanation

Krishna identifies himself with death that takes everything and with the arising of beings yet to be born. Among feminine powers he names fame, prosperity, speech, memory, intelligence, steadfastness and forgiveness. These qualities have feminine names and divine personifications. The declaration includes both the end and beginning of life and the distinguished powers named in the second half of the verse.

## 10.35

### Meaning

Among Sama chants I am the Brihat Saman; among metres, Gayatri. Among months I am Margashirsha, and among seasons, the flower-bearing spring.

### Explanation

The Brihat Saman is selected among Sama chants, Gayatri among metres, Margashirsha among months and spring among seasons. The last is literally the flower-bearing season. These particular chants, metrical forms and divisions of the year are Krishna's named manifestations within their classes. Sacred recitation and the recurring calendar continue the catalogue alongside its gods, creatures and human capacities.

## 10.36

### Meaning

Among deceivers I am gambling; I am the brilliance of the brilliant. I am victory, resolve and the goodness or strength of those endowed with it.

### Explanation

Krishna identifies himself with gambling among deceivers. He then names the brilliance of the brilliant, victory, determined effort and the sattva of those who possess it. Sattva here carries the senses of goodness, strength or vigour. Gambling and deception are stated directly among the manifestations, alongside successful and forceful human capacities.

## 10.37

### Meaning

Among the Vrishnis I am Vasudeva; among the Pandavas, Dhananjaya. Among sages I am Vyasa; among inspired poets, Ushanas.

### Explanation

Vasudeva names Krishna among the Vrishnis; Dhananjaya names Arjuna among the Pandavas. Vyasa is chosen among sages, and Ushanas among inspired poets. Krishna thus includes himself and the person he is addressing in the catalogue. The four names identify distinguished persons within lineages and classes of seers, continuing his account of principal manifestations.

## 10.38

### Meaning

Among those who discipline I am the rod of authority; among those seeking victory, sound policy. Among secrets I am silence, and among knowers, knowledge.

### Explanation

The rod, daṇḍa, is the instrument of punishment or discipline. Krishna identifies himself with it among those who govern through restraint, and with policy among those seeking victory. Silence is his identification among secrets, and knowledge among knowers. The verse brings authority, strategy, secrecy and understanding into the account of his manifestations.

## 10.39

### Meaning

Whatever is the seed of all beings, I am that, Arjuna. No moving or unmoving being exists that could be without me.

### Explanation

Krishna calls himself the seed of all beings and says that no moving or unmoving being can exist without him. The declaration extends across the whole world after the particular manifestations just listed. Seed names him as their source, while “without me” states their continuing dependence. Every being is included in this relation, beyond the distinguished examples individually named.

## 10.40

### Meaning

There is no end to my divine manifestations, scorcher of foes. This account of their extent has been given only by way of indication.

### Explanation

Krishna says that his divine manifestations have no end. The account just given is only an indication of their extent, repeating the qualification at 10.19. Arjuna asked for the full description; Krishna has named principal manifestations and now states their inexhaustibility. The following verse gives a rule encompassing further beings distinguished by splendour, prosperity or power.

## 10.41

### Meaning

Whatever being has distinctive splendour, beauty or force, understand that very thing to arise from a portion of my radiance.

### Explanation

The selection of examples becomes a broader rule of contemplation. Splendour, prosperity and vigorous power can direct attention toward the radiance from which they arise. Aṃśa, “portion,” gives the relation a language of participation without turning any single example into an exhaustive measure of Krishna. The claim also keeps the world's real distinctions of capacity visible: their prominence can serve recognition while their dependence remains fundamental.

## 10.42

### Meaning

Or what need have you of knowing all this in detail, Arjuna? Supporting this whole world with a single portion of myself, I remain.

### Explanation

The conclusion gathers the catalogue into one comprehensive statement. Krishna's sustaining presence exceeds the sum of the examples, and even the world as a whole is described through only a portion of his power. The question redirects attention from accumulating names to understanding that dependence. It also prepares Arjuna's next request: having heard the comprehensive claim, he will ask to see the divine form in which the world is held together.
