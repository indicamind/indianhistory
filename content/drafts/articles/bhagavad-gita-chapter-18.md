Krishna distinguishes renunciation from relinquishment, explains the causes of action, and classifies knowledge, action and the agent through the gunas. He sets out the duties of the four varnas and the attainment of Brahman, then commands devotion and refuge in him. Arjuna declares that he will obey. Sanjaya closes by affirming fortune and victory where Krishna and the bow-bearing Arjuna are present.

Each **Meaning** renders the Sanskrit in English. Each **Explanation** follows the verse’s statements, terms and argument. Named traditional interpretations are distinguished where they differ. [1–3]

## 18.1

### Meaning

Arjuna said: Mighty-armed Hrishikesha, destroyer of Keshi, I wish to know the truth of renunciation and relinquishment, each distinctly.

### Explanation

Arjuna asks Krishna to distinguish *saṃnyāsa*, renunciation, from *tyāga*, relinquishment. He wants the meaning of each stated separately. The question returns to a central issue of the dialogue: what is to be abandoned when one seeks liberation through knowledge, action and devotion.

## 18.2

### Meaning

The Blessed Lord said: The learned understand renunciation as laying aside actions undertaken for desired results. The discerning describe relinquishment as giving up the fruits of all actions.

### Explanation

Krishna gives two definitions. *Saṃnyāsa* is the abandonment of actions undertaken to obtain desired results; *tyāga* is the relinquishment of the fruits of all actions. The first definition concerns leaving particular actions, while the second concerns giving up their results.

## 18.3

### Meaning

Some thoughtful people say that action should be abandoned because it carries faults. Others say that sacrifice, giving and austerity should not be abandoned.

### Explanation

Krishna reports two views about abandoning action. Some regard action as faulty and therefore to be given up; others say sacrifice, giving and austerity must continue. His own judgement follows in 18.4–6, where he retains these practices and prescribes relinquishment of attachment and their fruits.

## 18.4

### Meaning

Hear my settled judgement on relinquishment, foremost of the Bharatas. Relinquishment, tiger among men, has been described as threefold.

### Explanation

Krishna asks Arjuna to hear his settled judgement about relinquishment. He announces that it is threefold. The following account distinguishes tamasic, rajasic and sattvic relinquishment according to delusion, fear of bodily hardship, and the performance of duty without attachment to its fruit.

## 18.5

### Meaning

Sacrifice, giving and austerity should not be abandoned; they should be practised. Sacrifice, giving and austerity are purifying for those who understand.

### Explanation

Sacrifice, giving and austerity are to be performed, not abandoned. Krishna gives their purifying effect as the reason. This answers the disagreement reported in 18.3 by affirming these three practices before specifying in 18.6 the relinquishment that should accompany them.

## 18.6

### Meaning

Even these actions should be performed while giving up attachment and their fruits. This is my firm and considered judgement, Partha.

### Explanation

Even sacrifice, giving and austerity are to be performed without attachment and without a claim to their fruits. Krishna states this as his firm judgement. Their continuation and the abandonment of attachment are therefore prescribed together.

## 18.7

### Meaning

Renunciation of prescribed action is not fitting. Abandoning it through delusion is described as tamasic.

### Explanation

Giving up prescribed action through delusion is called tamasic relinquishment. Krishna says that abandoning such action is not fitting. The description identifies both what is left aside—prescribed duty—and the deluded motive that makes its abandonment tamasic.

## 18.8

### Meaning

When someone gives up an action simply because it is painful, from fear of bodily hardship, that person performs rajasic relinquishment and does not gain the fruit of true relinquishment.

### Explanation

Relinquishment is rajasic when a person abandons an action because it is painful or brings bodily hardship. Krishna says this does not yield the fruit of relinquishment. Fear of suffering, rather than freedom from attachment, motivates the refusal to perform the action.

## 18.9

### Meaning

When prescribed action is performed simply because it ought to be done, Arjuna, relinquishing attachment and its fruit, that relinquishment is considered sattvic.

### Explanation

Sattvic relinquishment consists in performing prescribed action simply because it ought to be done, while giving up attachment and its fruit. The duty is carried out. What is abandoned is the attachment to doing it for oneself and claiming its result.

## 18.10

### Meaning

The relinquishing person who is filled with clarity, discerning and free from doubt neither hates disagreeable action nor clings to agreeable action.

### Explanation

The discerning renouncer, filled with sattva and free from doubt, neither hates disagreeable action nor clings to agreeable action. Attraction and aversion do not determine which duty is accepted. This describes the renouncer’s disposition after the definition of sattvic relinquishment in 18.9.

## 18.11

### Meaning

For an embodied person, it is impossible to abandon actions completely. The person who relinquishes the fruits of action is therefore called a relinquishing person.

### Explanation

Krishna says that an embodied person cannot abandon every action completely. He therefore calls the person who gives up the fruits of action a renouncer. The verse makes relinquishment of results the defining point while bodily activity continues.

## 18.12

### Meaning

The fruit of action is threefold: unwanted, wanted and mixed. After death it comes to those who have not relinquished, but never to the renouncers.

### Explanation

Action has three kinds of fruit: unwanted, wanted and mixed. Krishna says these come after death to those who have not relinquished, but never to renouncers. The distinction concerns the posthumous fruits of karma, and follows the definition of relinquishment in 18.11.

## 18.13

### Meaning

Learn from me, mighty-armed one, these five causes declared in the conclusive Sankhya analysis for the accomplishment of every action.

### Explanation

Krishna introduces five causes through which every action is accomplished. He attributes this analysis to Sankhya, the conclusive account of action and its causes. The five factors are named in 18.14 and then applied to actions of body, speech and mind.

## 18.14

### Meaning

The basis of action, the agent, the various instruments, the many different efforts, and the divine factor as the fifth—these are its causes.

### Explanation

The five causes are the bodily basis, the agent, instruments such as the senses, the various efforts including the vital-breath functions, and the divine factor. *Daiva* is explained in the commentaries through divine presiding powers or the Lord’s governance. The list prepares Krishna’s rejection of the self alone as the sole cause of action. [3]

## 18.15

### Meaning

Whatever action a person undertakes through body, speech or mind, whether right or its opposite, these five are its causes.

### Explanation

The five causes apply to every action undertaken through body, speech or mind, whether right or wrong. The classification includes mental and verbal acts as well as bodily deeds. Krishna presents the same fivefold causation for all of them.

## 18.16

### Meaning

Since this is so, the one who sees the isolated self alone as the agent does not truly see, because that person’s understanding has not been cultivated.

### Explanation

After naming five causes, Krishna rejects the view that the isolated self alone is the agent. He attributes that view to an uncultivated understanding and says that such a person does not truly see. The error is the attribution of action solely to the self.

## 18.17

### Meaning

The one whose disposition is free from egoistic claiming and whose understanding is unstained, even though killing these people, neither kills nor becomes bound.

### Explanation

Krishna says that one without egoistic claiming and with unstained understanding neither kills nor becomes bound, even while killing these people. Shankara explains this through the self’s ultimate non-agency; Ramanuja explains individual agency as dependent upon the indwelling Lord. The statement concerns both killing and the absence of karmic bondage. [3]

## 18.18

### Meaning

Knowledge, what is to be known and the knower form the threefold impulse to action. Instrument, action and agent form the threefold constitution of action.

### Explanation

Knowledge, its object and the knower form the threefold impulse that prompts action. Instrument, action and agent form the threefold constitution through which action is carried out. Krishna separates the knowing that precedes a deed from the means, deed and doer involved in its performance.

## 18.19

### Meaning

Knowledge, action and the agent are each said to be threefold according to the differences of the gunas in the analysis of qualities. Hear those distinctions as they truly are.

### Explanation

Krishna announces that knowledge, action and the agent each have three forms according to the gunas. The following verses describe each as sattvic, rajasic or tamasic. This applies the threefold classification to the elements of action introduced in 18.18.

## 18.20

### Meaning

Know as sattvic the knowledge by which one sees a single, imperishable reality in all beings, undivided within what appears divided.

### Explanation

Sattvic knowledge sees one imperishable reality in all beings, undivided amid their divisions. Shankara reads this as the one undivided self; Ramanuja reads it as the common imperishable nature of individual selves. Both explanations address the verse’s vision of unity, while differing about the identity of the selves. [3]

## 18.21

### Meaning

Know as rajasic the knowledge that sees in all beings many different realities, each as separately constituted.

### Explanation

Rajasic knowledge sees many distinct realities in beings and takes each as separate. It is contrasted with the sattvic knowledge of an undivided reality in 18.20. The distinction between the two kinds of knowledge is expressed through their perception of unity and separateness.

## 18.22

### Meaning

The knowledge that clings to one limited thing as if it were the whole, without sound reason, without grasping reality and confined in scope, is called tamasic.

### Explanation

Tamasic knowledge fastens on one limited thing as though it were the whole. Krishna describes it as lacking sound reason, failing to grasp reality and confined in scope. This completes the three kinds of knowledge, moving from undivided reality, through separate realities, to fixation on a fragment.

## 18.23

### Meaning

Action that is prescribed, free from attachment, performed without attraction or aversion by someone who does not seek its fruit, is called sattvic.

### Explanation

Sattvic action is prescribed duty performed without attachment, attraction, aversion or desire for its fruit. Krishna names both the kind of act and the disposition of its performer. This applies the preceding teaching of relinquishment to the classification of action itself.

## 18.24

### Meaning

Action performed with much strain by someone seeking desired objects, or acting with egoistic self-assertion, is called rajasic.

### Explanation

Rajasic action is performed for desired objects or from egoistic self-assertion and involves great exertion. Its strenuous performance is tied to craving or the agent’s assertion of “I.” These characteristics distinguish it from the prescribed, unattached action of 18.23.

## 18.25

### Meaning

Action undertaken through delusion, without regard for its consequences, loss, injury or one’s capacity, is called tamasic.

### Explanation

Tamasic action is undertaken in delusion without considering its consequences, loss, injury or the agent’s capacity. Krishna lists what the doer fails to consider before acting. Delusion and this disregard of the undertaking’s effects identify the action as tamasic.

## 18.26

### Meaning

An agent free from attachment and egoistic boasting, endowed with perseverance and enthusiasm, and unchanged by success or failure is called sattvic.

### Explanation

A sattvic agent is free from attachment and egoistic boasting, perseveres with enthusiasm and remains unchanged by success or failure. These qualities describe the doer, following the classification of action in 18.23–25. Detachment and equanimity are stated alongside perseverance and energy.

## 18.27

### Meaning

An agent who is attached, seeks the fruit of action, is greedy, injurious and impure, and is subject to elation and sorrow is described as rajasic.

### Explanation

A rajasic agent is attached, seeks the fruit, is greedy, injurious and impure, and alternates between elation and grief. Krishna joins desire for results with these qualities of conduct and response. The description contrasts with the unattached and even-minded agent of 18.26.

## 18.28

### Meaning

An agent who is uncollected, uncultivated, obstinate, deceitful, malicious, lazy, despondent and endlessly postponing is called tamasic.

### Explanation

The tamasic agent is uncollected, uncultivated, obstinate, deceitful, malicious, lazy, despondent and prone to endless delay. Krishna lists these as the characteristics of the doer. This completes the threefold classification of agents begun in 18.26.

## 18.29

### Meaning

Hear now the threefold distinction of understanding and perseverance according to the gunas, fully and separately explained, Dhananjaya.

### Explanation

Krishna turns to *buddhi*, understanding or discernment, and *dhṛti*, perseverance. Each is to be explained separately in its sattvic, rajasic and tamasic forms. The next three verses classify understanding, followed by three verses classifying perseverance.

## 18.30

### Meaning

The understanding that knows engagement and withdrawal, what should and should not be done, danger and security, bondage and liberation is sattvic, Partha.

### Explanation

Sattvic understanding distinguishes engagement from withdrawal, what should be done from what should not, danger from security, and bondage from liberation. These are the objects it knows correctly. The verse defines discernment by its ability to recognise these differences in conduct and attainment.

## 18.31

### Meaning

The understanding by which one recognises dharma and adharma, what should be done and what should not be done, inaccurately is rajasic, Partha.

### Explanation

Rajasic understanding recognises dharma and adharma, and what should or should not be done, inaccurately. Its error is a failure of correct discernment. This is contrasted with the clear discrimination of sattvic understanding and the reversal described as tamasic in 18.32.

## 18.32

### Meaning

The understanding that, covered by darkness, takes adharma to be dharma and sees all things in a reversed way is tamasic, Partha.

### Explanation

Tamasic understanding is covered by darkness and takes adharma to be dharma. It sees things in a reversed way. Krishna describes a direct inversion of right and wrong, completing the three forms of understanding.

## 18.33

### Meaning

The unwavering perseverance by which one holds the activities of mind, vital breaths and senses through yoga is sattvic, Partha.

### Explanation

Sattvic perseverance holds the activities of mind, vital breaths and senses steady through unwavering yoga. The object of perseverance is their sustained discipline. This begins the threefold account of *dhṛti*, following the three kinds of understanding.

## 18.34

### Meaning

The perseverance through which a person holds to dharma, pleasure and material gain, while attached and seeking their fruits, is rajasic, Arjuna.

### Explanation

Rajasic perseverance holds to dharma, pleasure and material gain while remaining attached and desiring their fruits. The three aims are named directly, and attachment to their results determines the classification. This is contrasted with the unwavering yogic discipline of 18.33.

## 18.35

### Meaning

The perseverance by which an undiscerning person will not let go of sleep, fear, grief, despondency and conceit is tamasic, Partha.

### Explanation

Tamasic perseverance keeps an undiscerning person fastened to sleep, fear, grief, despondency and conceit. The person does not let go of these states. This completes the three kinds of perseverance by naming what it maintains and the lack of discernment accompanying it.

## 18.36

### Meaning

Now hear from me of the three kinds of happiness, foremost of the Bharatas: that in which one comes to delight through practice and reaches the end of suffering—

### Explanation

Krishna introduces the three kinds of happiness. The first description begins with happiness in which one learns to delight through practice and reaches the end of suffering. Its account continues in 18.37, where its beginning, outcome and source are specified.

## 18.37

### Meaning

—the happiness that is like poison at first but like nectar in its outcome, born from the clarity of understanding of the self, is called sattvic.

### Explanation

Sattvic happiness is painful like poison at first but becomes like nectar in its outcome. It arises from clarity in the understanding of the self. The comparison joins the initial difficulty of the discipline with the happiness and end of suffering attained through it.

## 18.38

### Meaning

The happiness arising from the contact of senses and objects, like nectar at first but like poison in its outcome, is remembered as rajasic.

### Explanation

Rajasic happiness arises from the contact of the senses with their objects. It is pleasant like nectar at first but becomes like poison in its outcome. The sequence is the reverse of sattvic happiness: initial sweetness is followed by a harmful result.

## 18.39

### Meaning

The happiness that deludes the self both at the beginning and in its continuation, arising from sleep, indolence and heedlessness, is called tamasic.

### Explanation

Tamasic happiness deludes the self from the beginning and throughout its continuation. It arises from sleep, indolence and heedlessness. Krishna completes the classification by distinguishing this enduring delusion from the changing beginnings and outcomes of sattvic and rajasic happiness.

## 18.40

### Meaning

There is no being on earth, or again among the gods in heaven, that is free from these three gunas born of nature.

### Explanation

No being on earth or among the gods in heaven is free from the three gunas born of nature. The statement includes human and divine beings alike. It closes the preceding classification of knowledge, action, agents, understanding, perseverance and happiness before Krishna describes the duties of the four varnas.

## 18.41

### Meaning

The duties of brahmins, kshatriyas, vaishyas and shudras are divided according to the gunas arising from their own nature, scorcher of enemies.

### Explanation

Krishna names brahmins, kshatriyas, vaishyas and shudras and says that their duties are divided according to the gunas arising from their own nature. *Svabhāva* names that nature. The following three verses specify the work assigned to each of the four varnas.

## 18.42

### Meaning

Calm, sense-restraint, austerity, purity, patience, straightforwardness, knowledge, realised understanding and trust in sacred teaching are the brahmin’s work arising from nature.

### Explanation

The brahmin’s work comprises calm, control of the senses, austerity, purity, patience, straightforwardness, knowledge, realised understanding and faith in sacred teaching. Krishna describes these as arising from the brahmin’s nature. *Jñāna* and *vijñāna* distinguish knowledge from realised understanding; *āstikya* names affirmation of sacred teaching.

## 18.43

### Meaning

Courage, vigour, perseverance, capability, not retreating from battle, generosity and the capacity to govern are the kshatriya’s work arising from nature.

### Explanation

The kshatriya’s work includes courage, vigour, perseverance, capability, refusal to retreat from battle, generosity and the ability to govern. Krishna describes these as arising from the kshatriya’s nature. Not fleeing in battle is an explicit part of the duty of the varna to which Arjuna belongs.

## 18.44

### Meaning

Agriculture, the tending of cattle and trade are the vaishya’s work arising from nature. Work consisting in service is likewise the shudra’s work arising from nature.

### Explanation

Krishna assigns agriculture, cattle-tending and trade to the vaishya, and work consisting in service to the shudra. Both are described as arising from their respective natures. This completes the account of the four varnas’ work introduced in 18.41.

## 18.45

### Meaning

Devoted to their own respective work, a person attains fulfilment. Hear how one who is committed to one’s own work comes to fulfilment.

### Explanation

Devotion to one’s own work brings fulfilment. The statement follows the list of the four varnas and their duties. Krishna now explains how the performance of this respective work becomes a means to fulfilment, beginning with worship through work in 18.46.

## 18.46

### Meaning

By worshipping through one’s own work the one from whom beings proceed and by whom all this is pervaded, a person attains fulfilment.

### Explanation

One attains fulfilment by worshipping, through one’s own work, the being from whom all beings arise and by whom all this is pervaded. Work is thus made the means of worship. The verse explains the attainment promised to those devoted to their respective duties in 18.45.

## 18.47

### Meaning

One’s own dharma, though imperfectly performed, is better than another’s well performed. Doing work determined by one’s own nature, one does not incur wrongdoing.

### Explanation

Krishna ranks one’s own dharma, even when imperfectly performed, above another person’s dharma well performed. He adds that work determined by one’s own nature does not incur sin. In this sequence, the respective duties have been specified in 18.41–44 and made a form of worship in 18.46.

## 18.48

### Meaning

One should not abandon work born with one’s nature, son of Kunti, even when it has faults. Every undertaking is covered by some fault, as fire is covered by smoke.

### Explanation

Arjuna is told not to abandon work born with his nature, even when it has faults. Krishna compares the faults covering every undertaking to smoke covering fire. The presence of such faults is therefore not given as a reason to abandon one’s own work.

## 18.49

### Meaning

With understanding unattached everywhere, self-governed and free from craving, one attains through renunciation the highest fulfilment of freedom from action.

### Explanation

Krishna describes the highest fulfilment of freedom from action, attained through renunciation. Its conditions are understanding unattached everywhere, self-mastery and the absence of craving. *Naiṣkarmya-siddhi* names this fulfilment, which leads into the following account of attaining Brahman.

## 18.50

### Meaning

Learn briefly from me, son of Kunti, how one who has reached fulfilment attains Brahman, the supreme culmination of knowledge.

### Explanation

Krishna promises a brief account of how one who has attained fulfilment reaches Brahman. He calls this the supreme culmination of knowledge. The next three verses set out the disciplines and renunciations through which a person becomes fit for establishment in Brahman.

## 18.51

### Meaning

Endowed with purified understanding, governing oneself through perseverance, relinquishing sense objects such as sound, and setting aside attraction and aversion—

### Explanation

The practitioner has purified understanding, governs the self through perseverance, gives up sense objects such as sound, and relinquishes attraction and aversion. This begins a description continued through 18.53. The named disciplines concern understanding, self-restraint and withdrawal from objects of the senses.

## 18.52

### Meaning

—frequenting solitude, eating lightly, governing speech, body and mind, always intent on the yoga of meditation and firmly taking up dispassion—

### Explanation

The practitioner lives in solitude, eats lightly, restrains speech, body and mind, remains devoted to meditation, and takes up dispassion. These are the conditions of life and practice added to the description begun in 18.51. They continue the account of becoming fit for establishment in Brahman.

## 18.53

### Meaning

—releasing egoism, domineering power, arrogance, desire, anger and possessiveness, free from “mine” and at peace, one becomes fit for establishment in Brahman.

### Explanation

The practitioner gives up egoism, domineering power, arrogance, desire, anger and possessiveness. Free from the claim “mine” and at peace, such a person becomes fit for establishment in Brahman. This completes the sentence of 18.51–53.

## 18.54

### Meaning

Established in Brahman, with a clear and serene self, one neither grieves nor craves. Even toward all beings, one attains supreme devotion to me.

### Explanation

One established in Brahman is serene, neither grieves nor desires, and regards all beings equally. Krishna says that such a person attains supreme devotion to him. The verse joins the condition of Brahman-realisation with the attainment of the highest devotion.

## 18.55

### Meaning

Through devotion, one truly knows me—who I am and how great I am. Then, knowing me in truth, one enters me thereafter.

### Explanation

Through devotion, one knows Krishna in truth—both who he is and his greatness. Having known him in this way, one enters him. The verse states a sequence of devotion, true knowledge and entry into Krishna, continuing the promise of supreme devotion in 18.54.

## 18.56

### Meaning

Even while always performing all actions, one who takes refuge in me attains the eternal, imperishable state through my grace.

### Explanation

Krishna says that even a person who continues performing all actions attains the eternal, imperishable state by taking refuge in him. The attainment comes through his grace. This places refuge and grace at the centre of the promise while explicitly retaining the performance of action.

## 18.57

### Meaning

Mentally entrusting all actions to me, taking me as the highest aim and relying on the yoga of understanding, keep your attention constantly in me.

### Explanation

Arjuna is instructed to surrender all actions mentally to Krishna, make Krishna his highest aim, rely on buddhi-yoga and keep his mind constantly in him. Dedication of action, disciplined understanding and sustained attention are given together. The assurance and warning that follow concern this instruction.

## 18.58

### Meaning

With your attention in me, you will cross all difficulties through my grace. But if, through egoism, you refuse to listen, you will be lost.

### Explanation

Krishna promises that, with the mind fixed on him, Arjuna will cross all difficulties through his grace. He also warns that refusal to listen through egoism will bring ruin. The verse states both outcomes: deliverance through devotion and grace, and destruction through egoistic refusal.

## 18.59

### Meaning

If, relying on egoism, you think, “I will not fight,” that resolution of yours is futile. Your nature will impel you.

### Explanation

Krishna calls the egoistic resolution “I will not fight” futile. Arjuna’s nature will impel him to act. The refusal stated in 2.9 is thus answered directly: Krishna says that Arjuna’s own nature will overcome that decision.

## 18.60

### Meaning

Bound by your own work arising from your nature, son of Kunti, you will do even unwillingly what, through delusion, you now do not wish to do.

### Explanation

Arjuna is bound by work arising from his own nature. Krishna says that he will perform, even unwillingly, what he now refuses through delusion. The verse continues 18.59’s assertion that his nature will compel him to fight.

## 18.61

### Meaning

The Lord dwells in the heart of all beings, Arjuna, causing all beings to revolve through his maya, mounted upon a mechanism.

### Explanation

The Lord dwells in the heart of every being and causes beings to revolve through his maya as though mounted on a mechanism. The image describes divine governance of embodied beings. It follows the account of Arjuna being compelled by his own nature and precedes the command to take refuge in that Lord.

## 18.62

### Meaning

Take refuge in him alone with your whole being, descendant of Bharata. Through his grace, you will attain the highest peace and the abiding state.

### Explanation

Arjuna is told to take refuge in the indwelling Lord with his whole being. Through the Lord’s grace he will attain supreme peace and the abiding state. The instruction follows 18.61, where the Lord is described as dwelling in all hearts and directing beings through his maya.

## 18.63

### Meaning

Thus I have explained to you knowledge more hidden than the hidden. Reflect on it fully, and then act as you choose.

### Explanation

Krishna says that he has explained knowledge more secret than the secret and tells Arjuna to reflect on it fully. He then says, “Act as you choose.” The verse directly calls for consideration of the teaching before the decision to act.

## 18.64

### Meaning

Hear once more my supreme word, the most hidden of all. Because you are deeply dear to me, I will tell you what is for your good.

### Explanation

Krishna asks Arjuna to hear his supreme and most secret word once more. He gives his reason for telling it: Arjuna is deeply dear to him, and the teaching is for his good. The following two verses contain the final commands of devotion and refuge.

## 18.65

### Meaning

Keep your mind in me, become my devotee, worship me and bow to me. You will come to me alone: I truly promise you this, for you are dear to me.

### Explanation

Krishna commands Arjuna to keep his mind in him, become his devotee, worship him and bow to him. He promises that Arjuna will come to him alone, declaring the promise true because Arjuna is dear to him. The instruction repeats the devotional commands given in 9.34.

## 18.66

### Meaning

Relinquishing all dharmas, take refuge in me alone. I will liberate you from all wrongdoing; do not grieve.

### Explanation

Krishna commands the relinquishment of all dharmas and refuge in him alone, promising liberation from all sins. Shankara reads the relinquishment through abandonment of actions and establishment in knowledge of the self. Ramanuja explains surrender of agency and fruits while performing dedicated duties, and also the abandonment of burdensome expiatory rites in reliance on the Lord. Krishna concludes, “Do not grieve.” [3]

## 18.67

### Meaning

This teaching should never be given to one without austerity or devotion, to one unwilling to listen, or to one who finds fault with me.

### Explanation

Krishna forbids giving this teaching to four kinds of people: those without austerity, those without devotion, those unwilling to listen, and those who find fault with him. The prohibition concerns who may receive the teaching. The following verses describe the rewards of explaining, studying and hearing it.

## 18.68

### Meaning

The person who explains this highest secret among my devotees, practising the highest devotion to me, will certainly come to me.

### Explanation

One who explains this highest secret among Krishna’s devotees, with supreme devotion to Krishna, will certainly come to him. Teaching the dialogue is here itself joined to devotion. The promise concerns its explanation among devotees, following the restrictions on transmission in 18.67.

## 18.69

### Meaning

No one among human beings does a dearer service to me than that person, and no one on earth will be dearer to me.

### Explanation

Krishna says that no person does him a dearer service than the teacher described in 18.68, and that no one on earth will be dearer to him. The act being praised is explaining this highest secret among his devotees with devotion.

## 18.70

### Meaning

The one who studies this dharmic dialogue between us will, in my judgement, worship me through the sacrifice of knowledge.

### Explanation

Krishna regards the study of this dialogue concerning dharma as worship of him through the sacrifice of knowledge. Study is therefore named as a form of offering. It follows the praise of the person who explains the teaching to others.

## 18.71

### Meaning

Even the person who hears it with trust and without hostility, being freed, will attain the blessed worlds of those whose actions are meritorious.

### Explanation

Even one who hears the dialogue with faith and without hostility will be freed and attain the blessed worlds of those who perform meritorious deeds. Krishna extends the promise to the listener. Faith and absence of hostile fault-finding are the qualifications stated here.

## 18.72

### Meaning

Partha, have you heard this with single-pointed attention? Has your bewilderment born of ignorance been destroyed, Dhananjaya?

### Explanation

Krishna asks whether Arjuna has listened with undivided attention and whether his delusion born of ignorance has been destroyed. The question returns to the confusion about dharma Arjuna declared in 2.7. Arjuna’s answer in the next verse states what the teaching has accomplished.

## 18.73

### Meaning

Arjuna said: My delusion is gone, and through your grace I have recovered remembrance, Achyuta. I stand firm, free from doubt. I will do what you say.

### Explanation

Arjuna says that his delusion has been destroyed, that remembrance has returned through Krishna’s grace, and that he is firm and free from doubt. He declares, “I will do what you say.” His answer reverses his earlier refusal to fight.

## 18.74

### Meaning

Sanjaya said: Thus I heard this wondrous dialogue between Vasudeva and the great-souled Partha, making the hair stand on end.

### Explanation

Sanjaya resumes his account to Dhritarashtra after the dialogue between Krishna and Arjuna has ended. He calls their conversation wondrous and says that hearing it made his hair stand on end. Vasudeva names Krishna; Partha names Arjuna.

## 18.75

### Meaning

Through Vyasa’s grace I heard this supreme secret, this yoga, directly from Krishna, the Lord of yoga, speaking it himself.

### Explanation

Sanjaya attributes his hearing of this secret yoga directly from Krishna to Vyasa’s grace. Krishna is called the Lord of yoga and is the speaker himself. The verse states how Sanjaya received the dialogue that he has reported to Dhritarashtra.

## 18.76

### Meaning

King, as I remember again and again this wondrous, sacred dialogue between Keshava and Arjuna, I rejoice again and again.

### Explanation

Sanjaya tells the king that he rejoices repeatedly as he recalls the wondrous, sacred dialogue between Krishna and Arjuna. Keshava names Krishna. The repeated remembrance of their words brings the repeated joy described in the verse.

## 18.77

### Meaning

And as I remember again and again that exceedingly wondrous form of Hari, my amazement is great, king, and I rejoice again and again.

### Explanation

Sanjaya also repeatedly remembers Hari’s exceedingly wondrous form. This recalls the universal form revealed in Chapter 11. He says that the memory fills him with great amazement and renewed joy, adding the remembered vision to the remembered dialogue of 18.76.

## 18.78

### Meaning

Where Krishna, the Lord of yoga, is, and where Partha, the bearer of the bow, is, there are fortune, victory, flourishing and steadfast right policy. This is my conviction.

### Explanation

Sanjaya declares that where Krishna, Lord of yoga, and Arjuna, bearer of the bow, are present, there are fortune, victory, flourishing and steadfast right policy. He states this as his conviction. The Gita ends with his judgement linking Krishna’s presence and Arjuna’s armed action with these results.
