# Laghman II source notes (not a publishable article)

**Site and object.** Davary found this ten-line Aramaic-script inscription on
the Sam Baba ridge in the Laghman valley in 1973. Falk reproduces Davary's
photograph and a line drawing on printed p. 250, and describes the text on
p. 249. See [Falk, *Aśokan Sites and Artefacts* (2006)](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.3403874).

**Edition.** G. D. Davary and H. Humbach, *Eine weitere aramäoiranische
Inschrift der Periode des Aśoka aus Afghanistan* (1974), pp. 8–12, contains
the image, drawing, Roman transcription, and an interpretation. The complete
Roman transcription has not yet been verified against this edition. A partial
reproduction from a drawing would risk assigning the wrong consonants to
weathered marks, so do not generate a Unicode Aramaic text from the image
alone.

**Secure points.** Falk dates the inscription to Priyadarśin's year 16 and
quotes its opening as a month-of-Elul dating formula. The record also refers
to the king, local landmarks and measurements, and a person called Vasu near
the end. The last line contains another disputed personal name. The exact
meaning of the middle and final lines is unsettled.

**Competing readings.** Falk (pp. 249–250) prints Delaunay's French rendering
and Itō's very different English rendering side by side. Delaunay interprets
the central lines as rejecting injury to fish and living beings and then
describing a hill, road, and distances. Itō interprets the final lines as
travellers' guides and the distribution of alms. Falk notes Rosenthal's
alternative view that the parallel Laghman I might be a legal verdict dated
under Ashoka, rather than a decree authored by him. The eventual article
must present these disagreements without stitching their speculative words
into a single purported translation.

credited to Davary and Davary–Humbach. The Zenodo record permits access to the
book but does not state a reuse license for those figures. Link to Falk's
