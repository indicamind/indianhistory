# Gita: direct English explanation revision

[Review Chapter 1, verse 41](http://localhost:4201/scriptures/bhagavad-gita/1?dev=true&lang=en#verse-1-41) · [Chapter 9, verse 32](http://localhost:4201/scriptures/bhagavad-gita/9?dev=true&lang=en#verse-9-32) · [Chapter 18, verse 17](http://localhost:4201/scriptures/bhagavad-gita/18?dev=true&lang=en#verse-18-17).

All 701 explanations across 18 chapters were audited following the request to keep explanations close to their meanings without apologism or modern sensitivity. The revised English drafts change **574 explanations** and retain **127**. Chapter introductions now summarise the teachings directly. Five softened Meaning passages also have more direct renderings, listed below.

The revised manuscripts are `content/drafts/articles/bhagavad-gita-chapter-01.md` through `bhagavad-gita-chapter-18.md`. Published English manuscripts and translations remain unchanged. Sanskrit, IAST, all verse identifiers, source hashes, frontmatter metadata, Sources sections and citation markers are preserved. Chapter 13 retains the edition’s numbered opening question and 35-verse count.

## Editorial approach

Each explanation follows the verse’s claims, commands, named terms and argument. The revision removes modern reassurance, defensive qualifications, generic advice to readers, and psychological accounts substituted for literal doctrine. It states sacrifice, divine manifestation, destruction of wrongdoers, battle commands, varna duties, ritual eligibility, heaven, hell, rebirth and liberation directly. Statements of compassion, equal vision, non-injury and welfare are likewise retained wherever the verses state them.

Named traditional interpretations remain where they explain a real difference of reading. Independent review checked these attributions and corrected the draft’s attribution at local 13.23: Ramanuja applies its titles to the individual self superior to the body, rather than identifying a separate indwelling Lord in that particular verse. The draft also preserves non-doership at 4.20 and avoids adding “other people” to the causation statement at 5.13. [6–7]

Examples of the revised approach:

- **1.41:** Explains the stated sequence from adharma to corruption of the family’s women and intermixture of varnas. Removes the instruction about avoiding application to contemporary life.
- **4.8:** States protection of the good, destruction of wrongdoers and establishment of dharma, replacing the earlier generalisation about overcoming destructive conduct.
- **9.32:** Retains the verse’s wording about sinful birth, women, vaishyas and shudras, together with the distinct, sourced Shankara and Shridhara readings.
- **11.32–34:** States Krishna’s identity as Time, the destruction of warriors and the commands to fight and kill those he has already slain.
- **18.17:** States the claim that the one without egoistic claiming and with unstained understanding neither kills nor becomes bound even while killing these people. Retains the sourced non-agency and dependent-agency interpretations without adding an ethical reassurance.
- **18.41–48:** Explains the four varnas’ duties and the preference for one’s own dharma directly.
