## Evidence and scope

The bubbles cover distinct life events, documented government and diplomacy, religious or artistic patronage, and relevant historical context. They do not pretend that fifty independently witnessed incidents survive for every ruler. Primary chronicles remain attributed accounts; court praise is identified as such. Buddhist and Jain canonical narratives, later Rajput traditions, and disputed authorship or dates are labelled rather than silently converted into contemporary facts. Each manuscript ends with its numbered Sources and existing image credits.

The research used public-domain primary texts and historical editions alongside archaeological, manuscript, museum and institutional records. These include the Akbarnama, Baburnama, Jahangir’s account, Afif and the Futuhat, Greek accounts of the Mauryan world, named Buddhist discourses and Jain texts, Gandhi’s collected writings and chronology, and archival material for Tipu. Later Mewar histories are compared with contemporary sources, and romantic details remain attributed. Source lists in each manuscript identify the particular works used.

The review also corrects inherited draft problems, including the attribution of Gandhi’s role in the Poona Pact, the chronology of Tipu’s contact with Napoleon, and the distinction between commissioning the Rajprashasti and engraving it after Raj Singh’s death. Existing chapter identifiers and article associations are retained. Images already present, including Shivaji’s illustrated timeline, are preserved. This expansion does not add images to the new bubbles.

## Evidence-limited timelines

- **Bindusara — 19 bubbles.** Greek notices preserve diplomatic contacts and scattered details. Buddhist and Jain narratives are later and often concern Ashoka or the dynasty. No contemporary royal narrative supports filling the gap with dozens of individual events. The existing manuscript already separates those traditions and uncertainties.
- **Jaimal — 13 bubbles.** The strongest narrative evidence concerns the 1567–1568 Chittor siege; later regional histories and commemorative traditions supply a limited wider biography. Splitting that one episode into dozens of repetitions would misrepresent the breadth of the record.
- **Patta — 12 bubbles.** The documented centre is likewise the Chittor siege and its later remembrance. The reviewed material does not substantiate a fifty-entry personal chronology.

These counts are evidence-based exceptions, not completed fifty-bubble expansions. The existing Sources in their manuscripts record the material available for review. They can be expanded further if additional reliable evidence is located.
